04 Psychology

Adults — new, evidence-based findings

Adult ADHD linked to markedly higher later-life dementia risk

A University of Pittsburgh team analysing 187,341 adults aged 50+ in the NIH "All of Us" database found 6.7% of those with ADHD developed dementia or cognitive impairment versus 3.4% without, an adjusted risk more than fourfold higher. The study is observational and does not prove causation, but the association persisted even with an 8-year gap between ADHD diagnosis and cognitive decline. (DSM-5: ADHD.)

VerifiedJAMA Psychiatry · 7 Oct 2026
Sources:upmc.com · healio.com

Loneliness and isolation cost years of mental-health-free life

A Tulane-led analysis of 277,489 UK Biobank adults found that people who were both lonely and socially isolated lost substantial disease-free years — men about 5.8 years free of mental disorders, women about 3.7. A healthier lifestyle offset isolation's physical-disease effect but not the mental-health risk tied to loneliness. (DSM-5: depressive & anxiety disorders.)

PartialNature Communications · Aug–Sep 2026
Sources:nautil.us · news-medical.net

Children — the ~6-year-old brain (for Andrei, b. 2020)

Executive function surges as the prefrontal cortex matures

Between about five and seven, the prefrontal cortex and its networks mature quickly, driving gains in working memory, impulse control and sustained attention — the core of "school readiness." Practically, this is the year sitting still, waiting turns and following multi-step instructions become realistic expectations.

Theory of mind consolidates — "knowing about knowing"

By around age six children reliably reason about others' beliefs and perspectives, including understanding that people can be mistaken — a shift tied to prefrontal activity. Expect better perspective-taking, more complex cooperative play, and the start of genuine empathy-based sharing.

Verifieddevelopmental reference

Literacy and numeracy leap; magical thinking fades — and what to watch

Most six-year-olds begin decoding and reading, grasp counting and the concept of ten, and reason more about cause and effect while shedding preschool "magical thinking"; motor skills and friendships grow in importance. Flag for a paediatrician any developmental regression, persistent trouble following simple instructions, ongoing anxiety or aggression, social withdrawal, or vision/hearing concerns — the CDC milestones are "most children" guides, not a screening test.

06 Medicine

First personalised mRNA cancer vaccine passes a Phase 3 trial (melanoma)

In the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial (over 1,100 high-risk resected-melanoma patients), Moderna and Merck's individualised neoantigen vaccine intismeran plus pembrolizumab cut the risk of recurrence or death by 42% versus pembrolizumab alone. Billed as the first positive Phase 3 for an individualised neoantigen therapy and the first mRNA cancer treatment to succeed in a late-stage trial; it is not yet FDA-approved and overall-survival data are still maturing.

VerifiedESMO · 23 Oct 2026
Sources:scimex.org · ajmc.com

A new immune pathway against small-cell lung cancer

WEHI researchers reported that gamma-delta T cells — a previously overlooked immune cell type — can detect and kill small-cell lung cancer even when the tumour is "invisible" to the cells current immunotherapies rely on. Patients with more of these cells fared better, and existing drugs might boost their activity, pointing to a new avenue for one of the deadliest cancers.

PartialCancer Cell · 14–16 Oct 2026
Source:news-medical.net

Human cells "de-aged" in the lab

University of Bonn researchers converted blood-cell precursors from an 80-year-old donor directly into neural stem cells whose epigenetic age measured below 20, with the rejuvenation occurring gradually through the conversion. It is a laboratory research model — for example for studying Alzheimer's — not a treatment.

VerifiedAging Cell · 9 Oct 2026
Sources:uni-bonn.de · news-medical.net

Exercise linked to slower Parkinson's progression

A Neurology study following 294 people with mild-to-moderate Parkinson's for up to seven years found that higher weekly physical activity tracked with markedly slower cognitive and motor decline, with benefit appearing to plateau above roughly eight hours a week. The data are observational and based on self-reported activity.

VerifiedNeurology · 7 Oct 2026
Sources:uc.edu · powershealth.org

2026 Nobel Prize in Medicine goes to optogenetics

Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel shared the prize for discoveries on light-gated ion channels and optogenetics — using light to switch individual neurons on and off. The technique is now central to neuroscience, with potential for restoring vision and treating brain disorders.

Verifiedannounced 5 Oct 2026

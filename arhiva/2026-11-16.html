01 Dental

Medical & scientific discoveries

Pure cranberry juice may slow dental plaque and acid

A small University of Illinois Chicago study (randomised crossover, 8 adults and 14 children) found that 100% unsweetened cranberry juice slowed the regrowth of plaque bacteria and cut enamel-eroding acid output over a 30-minute window; the sugary 27% cranberry cocktail showed no benefit. The authors stress it is early, short-term work and does not prove it prevents cavities.

VerifiedCoverage 21–28 Oct 2026 · Scientific Reports
Sources: News-Medical · ScienceDaily · Medical Dialogues

Why antibiotics fail in peri-implantitis: titanium particles (background)

Rutgers researchers (PNAS Nexus) described a mechanism in which bacteria corrode titanium implants, shedding microscopic particles that blunt the ability of immune macrophages to clear infection — distinct from classic antibiotic resistance. Note: the original paper is from March 2026 and only resurfaced in late-October coverage, so this is background rather than fresh news. A separate small pilot study also reported amoxicillin/metronidazole resistance in peri-implant bacteria.

PartialOriginal Mar 2026 · re-shared 23 Oct 2026
Sources: ScienceDaily · Dental Tribune

Common mental disorders are lasting longer than a decade ago

A Psychological Medicine study using the Dutch NEMESIS survey compared cohorts starting in 2007 and 2019, each tracked over three years, and found DSM-defined mood, anxiety and substance-use disorders now persist longer. As reported, anxiety persistence rose from about 24% to 40%, and mood disorders from about 34% to 49%; rising individual risk factors did not fully explain it, pointing to broader societal drivers.

Children — the brain of a ~6-year-old (for Andrei, b. 2020)

What is developing around age 6

By about age 6 the brain is close to adult size (~95% of adult weight), but the prefrontal cortex — which governs self-regulation, planning and impulse control — is still immature and keeps developing into early adulthood. Researchers identify roughly 6–7 years as a developmental "watershed" for executive functions: working memory (holding in mind what to do) and inhibitory control (resisting what not to do). Synaptic pruning and myelination drive faster processing, longer attention and the ability to follow multi-step instructions.

VerifiedDevelopmental reference
Sources: Diamond, prefrontal development · Fiske & Holmboe (2019), early executive function

Milestones & red flags for the coming year

Note: the CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early" checklists stop at age 5, so the 5-year list plus the 6-year well-child visit are the authoritative references. Typical for a 6-year-old: speaking in complete 5–7-word sentences, beginning to sound out unfamiliar words, more confident gross-motor skills (hopping, catching, skipping), and more self-regulation with occasional meltdowns still normal. Raise with a pediatrician any loss of previously acquired skills (a red flag at any age), persistent difficulty reading or sounding out words, trouble with age-expected motor skills, or speech that unfamiliar adults cannot understand. These prompt screening, not a diagnosis.

05 Education

The next ~20 years vs. classical schooling

OECD's four scenarios for schooling to 2040

The OECD's foresight report sets out four alternative futures for schooling by about 2040: schooling extended (traditional structures intensify), education outsourced (learning moves to market arrangements), schools as learning hubs (schools repurposed, not abolished), and learn-as-you-go (school-based learning as we know it ends). The OECD frames these as thinking tools, not predictions, and notes "the place called school" still dominates today.

VerifiedOECD foresight
Source: OECD iLibrary

Lifelong learning becomes the organising idea

Across the OECD's Trends Shaping Education 2025 and Education Policy Outlook 2025 (drawing on 230+ policies across 35 economies), the consistent conclusion is a shift away from one-off, front-loaded schooling toward continuous, adaptable, lifelong learning as essential for economic resilience and social inclusion — even as participation in learning beyond initial education has stagnated. The WEF's Future of Jobs 2025 reinforces the same direction, stressing continuous reskilling.

How to educate a child of ~6 (evidence-based)

What the evidence most consistently supports

The best-supported principles for this age: a warm, secure attachment as the foundation for learning; emotion coaching (naming and validating feelings to build self-regulation); social coaching of friendship skills through cooperative and imaginative play; active support for vocabulary, pre-literacy and early reading (sounding out words); and calm, consistent limit-setting followed by relationship repair and modelling. The general principles are well supported across review sources; specific branded-program claims should be attributed rather than treated as proven outcomes.

Boehringer Ingelheim's survodutide, a glucagon/GLP-1 dual agonist, met its primary cardiovascular-safety endpoint in the phase 3 SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT trial, showing no increase in major adverse cardiovascular events versus placebo — which the company calls a first for this drug class. Drug advertising can sway AI treatment recommendations

Mount Sinai researchers tested 12 major AI models on identical clinical cases, with and without a labelled real drug advertisement placed before the case. The advertised drug was recommended about 48% of the time when an ad was present versus about 35% without — with no measurable drop in overall clinical accuracy. The authors frame it as a regulatory question for how pharma-advertising rules apply to AI health tools.

Verified11 Nov 2026 · Lancet Digital Health

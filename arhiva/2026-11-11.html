Psychology

Adults — recent findings (DSM-5 relevant)

Late-life low mood may follow early Alzheimer's brain changes

A Journal of Neuroscience study of 390 cognitively healthy older adults found rising tau protein tends to precede — and predict — a later rise in mild depressive symptoms, with little sign that mood changes drive tau build-up. This reframes some late-life low mood as a possible downstream marker of early neurodegeneration rather than a trigger, relevant to distinguishing a depressive disorder from a neurocognitive prodrome under DSM-5. The authors stress longer studies are needed.

Partial~7 Nov 2026
Sources: PsyPost · Neuroscience News

Authoritarian parenting and teens' antisocial beliefs — a between-family link

A longitudinal community study (Merrin et al., 2026) found teens of more authoritarian parents held modestly stronger antisocial beliefs on average, but baseline parenting did not predict changes in an individual teen's beliefs, anxiety or depression over time. The association looks like a stable between-family difference rather than evidence that authoritarian parenting worsens a given child's thinking; effects were modest.

Partial~8 Nov 2026
Sources: PsyPost · Author publication list

Children — Andrei (6, born 2020): brain development & what to watch

A cognitive leap: reasoning, memory and self-control

Around age six, children make marked gains in problem-solving, working memory, attention and following multi-step instructions, as the prefrontal cortex drives executive function (planning, impulse control, task-switching). Most start to enjoy rules and routines and to grasp cause-and-effect and simple time/number concepts — so clear structure and multi-step tasks suit this year well.

VerifiedReference
Sources: CDC milestones · Sesame Workshop

Social-emotional shift — toward peers and teachers

Six-year-olds increasingly look to friends and admired adults, can cooperate in play with two or three peers for 20+ minutes, and put feelings into words far better — while often worrying about being liked. Supporting friendships, naming emotions and steady reassurance matter most this year. Fine-motor skills also sharpen: tying laces, cutting along a line, dressing independently.

VerifiedReference
Sources: GreatSchools · Columbia Doctors

What to watch for this year

Milestones describe what most children do, and children vary — steady progress across domains matters more than any single skill. Worth raising with a pediatrician: persistent trouble with multi-step directions or focus well beyond peers; little interest in peers or cooperative play; marked difficulty expressing feelings or frequent uncontrolled aggression; clear lags in early reading/number readiness at school; or loss of previously held skills. At six the main channels are well-child visits and teacher feedback, so flag concerns proactively.

Education

The next ~20 years vs. the classical school

OECD's four scenarios for schooling to 2040

The OECD frames four plausible futures rather than one: (1) Schooling extended — formal schools keep expanding and certificates stay the main passport; (2) Education outsourced — learning disperses to diverse market/community providers; (3) Schools as learning hubs — schools persist but open up and flexibly anchor communities; (4) Learn-as-you-go — AI and digital tech dissolve the boundary between school and life. Classical schooling endures most in scenarios 1 and 3 and erodes in 2 and 4.

Verified (reference)OECD framework, reaffirmed 2025
Sources: OECD — Back to the Future(s) · Trends Shaping Education 2025

Educating a child of Andrei's age (6)

Guided play works as well as direct instruction — sometimes better

A University of Cambridge systematic review and meta-analysis (Child Development, 2022) across ages ~1–8 found "guided play" — a child exploring while an adult gently steers toward a learning goal — matched or beat direct instruction, with the clearest gains in early maths, shape knowledge and task-switching, and no outcome where it did worse. Practical takeaway: teach letters, numbers and problem-solving through purposeful games, building and real-life tasks rather than drilling.

Verified2022 (best evidence)
Sources: University of Cambridge · Neuroscience News

Match the method to the stage

At six, lean on his new love of rules and routine (consistent structure, clear expectations), his peer orientation (cooperative games, group learning) and growing executive function (multi-step tasks he can plan). Build emotional literacy by naming feelings, protect daily play and movement, keep screens purposeful, and read together every day.

Medicine

New discoveries & firsts (scientifically proven)

FDA approves asundexian (Sunharbo) — first Factor XIa inhibitor for stroke prevention

The FDA approved Bayer's asundexian, a first-in-class oral Factor XIa inhibitor, for use alongside antiplatelet therapy to cut the risk of recurrent stroke after a recent mild-to-moderate non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke or high-risk TIA. Approval rests on the Phase 3 OCEANIC-STROKE trial, which showed a 26% reduction in ischemic-stroke risk over a median ~14 months without a significant rise in major bleeding. It is not approved for atrial-fibrillation-related stroke prevention.

Verified9 Nov 2026
Sources: NeurologyLive · Fierce Pharma · Medscape

Merck's efinopegdutide hits histology endpoints in Phase 2b MASH trial

Merck reported positive Phase 2b results for efinopegdutide, a once-weekly GLP-1/glucagon dual agonist, in adults with MASH and stage F2–F3 fibrosis, presented at The Liver Meeting and published simultaneously in The Lancet. At 52 weeks, MASH resolution without worsening fibrosis reached 56–63% across doses versus 13% on placebo (all p<0.0001), with fibrosis improvement in up to 52% of patients and a safety profile consistent with GLP-1 therapies.

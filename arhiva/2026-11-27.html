Psychology

Adults — one evidence-based finding (DSM-5)

Building positive affect can outperform "fixing the negative" in depression and anxiety

A multisite, assessor-blinded randomized trial (98 adults with low positive affect plus depression and/or anxiety) compared 15 telehealth sessions of Positive Affect Treatment — training patients to notice, savor and build rewarding experiences — against a conventional approach targeting threat and negative feelings. The positive-affect arm produced significantly greater improvement in overall clinical status, with the authors pointing to modulation of the brain's reward and threat systems as the mechanism.

DSM-5 link: Participants carried DSM-5 diagnoses (major depression, generalized and social anxiety disorder), with anhedonia — a core DSM-5 depression symptom — measured via the SCID-5. The approach targets the low-positive-affect dimension that cuts across DSM-5 mood and anxiety disorders.

VerifiedPublished Apr 24, 2026
Sources: JAMA Network Open (DOI) · ClinicalTrials.gov
This April-2026 randomized trial is the most recent high-quality, DSM-5-relevant study fitting the brief; no credible new study surfaced strictly within the last 24–72h. A popular-press claim that the therapy also reduced suicidal behavior could not be confirmed in the primary article.

Children — Andrei's brain at ~6 (born 2020)

What is developing now

By age six the brain has reached roughly 90–95% of adult volume, so the gains now are in efficiency and wiring rather than size: myelination speeds signal transmission and the prefrontal cortex matures, supporting better impulse control, planning and attention. Cognitively this is the documented shift from preschool "magical" thinking toward logic, cause-and-effect reasoning and a firmer grasp of time, while reading, writing and multi-step instruction-following accelerate.

VerifiedDevelopmental norms
Sources: Kaiser Permanente · Scholastic

What to watch for in the coming year

Expect more cooperative play, turn-taking, rule-following and the ability to name feelings — but self-regulation is still immature and breaks down under stress (such as starting school), so big emotional reactions remain normal, with the parent acting as co-regulator. Flag for a pediatrician if you see loss of previously acquired skills, persistent trouble following simple instructions or focusing, little interest in other children, or reading/learning well behind classmates after a year of instruction. Standardized CDC milestone checklists stop at age 5, so rely on well-visits and school screening beyond that.

VerifiedDevelopmental norms
Sources: CDC · APA · Understood

Education

The next ~20 years vs. classical schooling

OECD's four scenarios for schooling in 2040

The OECD's "Back to the Future of Education" sets out four divergent futures: schools extended and more digital; education outsourced to fragmented private and community providers; schools reinvented as open community learning hubs; and "learn-as-you-go," where AI-powered learning is embedded in life and formal structures largely dissolve. The explicit message: traditional whole-cohort schooling is only one of several plausible outcomes.

VerifiedOECD report
Source: OECD

AI may compress how much formal education is needed

An OECD working paper (Nov 2025) models futures by how fast AI advances on basic versus professional capabilities; under rapid progress on both, it argues the duration and approach of formal education could need to change substantially, with credential-based qualifications giving way to demonstrable skills. It is scenario-modeling, not a point forecast.

VerifiedNov 2025
Source: OECD working paper

From pilots to system-wide AI tutoring

Sector forecasts frame the coming period as the move from pilot AI use to system-wide integration, with adaptive "intelligent tutoring systems" giving on-demand practice and feedback and freeing teacher time. Academic reviews find such systems produce measurable learning gains versus traditional instruction, though many long-range predictions are vendor-adjacent opinion.

Partial2026 forecasts
Source: eSchool News · NCBI review of intelligent tutoring systems

Educating a child Andrei's age (~6)

Play-based learning plus daily reading aloud

American Academy of Pediatrics guidance holds that unstructured and pretend play, reading aloud daily, and back-and-forth conversation during routines are what build school readiness, language and later reading — and that pushing formal academics before a child is ready can sap the enjoyment that drives learning. Practically: give time and simple props for imaginative play, read together as a fun family habit, and talk with (not just at) the child.

VerifiedAAP guidance
Source: HealthyChildren (AAP)

Support emotion regulation by modeling and co-regulating

At around six, children learn self-control largely by copying what parents do, not what they say, and they lose access to regulation skills when anxious — so the parent's role is calm modeling, naming feelings and co-regulating big emotions rather than expecting independent control. Regular family talk about emotions and simple age-appropriate mindfulness help; persistent struggles warrant a behavioral-health professional.

VerifiedAPA guidance
Source: APA · PBS Parents

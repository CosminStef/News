Medical discoveries & research

Jaw osteonecrosis common in bone-metastatic breast cancer on denosumab

A nationwide University of Oulu registry study in Finland — described as the first of its kind there — tracked 652 breast-cancer patients treated with bone-protecting drugs and found medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw in about 12.6% of those on long-term denosumab. The authors stress that necessary dental care should not be withheld, and call for closer coordination between oncology and dentistry.

Verified Oct 23, 2026
Sources: MedicalXpress, Dental Tribune (Clinical Oral Investigations)

Remineralisation gel forms hydroxyapatite from calcium hypophosphite

A BDJ Open paper reports a formulation that builds tooth-like hydroxyapatite directly on enamel in the presence of saliva, using calcium hypophosphite. The work points toward non-invasive repair of early enamel lesions, though it remains laboratory-stage.

Partial Oct 16, 2026
Source: BDJ Open (Nature) — single journal listing

Salivary thiol test explored for gum disease

A cross-sectional study in Clinical and Experimental Dental Research found that salivary thiol levels track with periodontitis-associated bacteria, suggesting a possible chair-side point-of-care test to help rule out harmful subgingival dysbiosis.

Partial Oct 15, 2026

04 Psychology

Adults — a recent, evidence-based finding

“AI psychosis”: rare but real, and hardest on first-episode patients

A Vanderbilt University Medical Center study searched records of about 215,000 mental-health patients and identified 28 cases (~0.013%) where conversational chatbots were tied to worsening psychotic symptoms; roughly 61% were people in their first psychotic episode. The authors say chatbots mostly amplified existing delusions rather than creating them, and that the design cannot prove causation. In DSM-5 terms it touches the schizophrenia-spectrum and first-episode psychosis.

Verified coverage Oct 23, 2026
Sources: PsyPost, JAMA Psychiatry (doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2026.2923)

Depression may be five distinct brain-connectivity conditions

A University of Helsinki team used magnetoencephalography on 263 people with major depressive disorder and 75 controls and identified five distinct brain-connectivity profiles, each with a different symptom pattern. The finding suggests the single DSM-5 category of “major depression” may mask several conditions, though the modest sample needs replication.

Verified Oct 8, 2026 (slightly older)
Sources: ScienceDaily, Helsinki Times (Nature Mental Health)

Children — Andrei (born 2020, turning 6): what’s developing

Established developmental science

Entering “middle childhood”: what to expect and what to watch

Around age six a child enters what the CDC calls middle childhood, a stretch of rapid mental growth: real reading and writing begin, counting gives way to understanding what numbers mean, cause-and-effect reasoning sharpens, earlier “magical thinking” fades, and attention span lengthens (a focused ~15-minute task is a common benchmark). Socially, he will want more independence while friends and admired teachers grow in importance, with better — but not complete — control of his emotions; school becomes his main window on the wider world.

Worth flagging to a paediatrician over the coming year if persistent: trouble making or keeping friends, very limited vocabulary or difficulty forming sentences, marked trouble following classroom routines, or persistent struggles with early reading and number concepts. Every source adds the same caveat — each child develops at his own pace.

Verified CDC (2024), AAP-aligned milestone guidance
Sources: CDC — Middle Childhood (6–8), Columbia Doctors / Kaiser Permanente milestone pages (AAP HealthyChildren)

05 Education

The next ~20 years vs. classical schooling

OECD’s four scenarios for the future of schooling

The main authoritative framework is the OECD’s four scenarios to around 2040 — explicitly planning tools, not predictions: (1) Schooling extended, in which today’s standardised, school-based systems modernise and persist; (2) education outsourced to diverse market providers; (3) schools as learning hubs that open up to community and digital partners; and (4) a learn-as-you-go world where ubiquitous tech dissolves the school as the unit of learning. The OECD’s 2025 trends work highlights AI-driven personalised learning and reframes goals around competencies rather than content.

Verified OECD/WEF, 2020–2025
Sources: OECD — Four Scenarios, WEF, OECD Trends Shaping Education 2025

Educating a child of Andrei’s age

Evidence-based guidance

Shared reading, named emotions, protected sleep, and play

The American Academy of Pediatrics’ 2024 literacy policy — its first update in a decade — frames daily shared, dialogic reading as a positive parenting practice that builds language, cognitive and social-emotional foundations: make books part of the bedtime routine and ask about characters’ feelings and choices. Alongside that, support his emerging emotional regulation by naming feelings and using play and role-play, protect a consistent bedtime, and stay engaged with his school, since independence and school are now central. Play — including fantasy play — should remain a primary vehicle for learning.

Verified AAP (2024), CDC (2024)
Sources: AAP literacy policy, CDC Positive Parenting Tips (6–8)

06 Medicine

Blood antibody patterns may flag inflammatory bowel disease a decade early

Researchers analysing 2,000 stored blood samples from 500 people found that antibody diversity was already lower in those who later developed Crohn’s disease or ulcerative colitis — a detectable signature up to ten years before diagnosis, with machine-learning models separating future cases from controls. The authors call it a proof of principle, not yet a validated screening test.

Verified UEG Week, ~Oct 17–19, 2026
Sources: EurekAlert, News-Medical, MedicalXpress

Low-dose aspirin linked to much lower dementia risk — in one genetic subgroup

A Monash University analysis of genetic data from more than 13,500 ASPREE-trial participants found that among the ~20% with a high platelet-genetics score, low-dose aspirin was associated with about 70% lower relative dementia risk. The same subgroup faces higher serious-bleeding risk, and the authors stress this is a hypothesis-generating subgroup analysis that needs a dedicated trial.

Verified re-featured Oct 24–25, 2026
Sources: ScienceDaily, Newsweek (Alzheimer’s & Dementia)

How pancreatic cancer rewrites its DNA to evade treatment

An NYU Langone / UC San Diego team profiled over 440,000 single cells from 62 tumours and showed that pancreatic-cancer cells keep evolving, with aggressive clones ramping up the MYC gene. In the lab, blocking MYC reverted aggressive cells to a milder state and made them more visible to the immune system — a mechanistic finding, with no clinical benefit shown yet.

Verified published Oct 13; re-featured Oct 24, 2026
Sources: ScienceDaily, News-Medical (Nature Genetics)

Ear-nerve stimulation for stubborn high blood pressure: mostly a negative trial

The sham-controlled SCRATCH-HTN trial of a home ear-clip neuromodulation device in 63 patients found no significant blood-pressure benefit versus sham overall, though a pre-specified subgroup with more severe hypertension did better. The device was safe and well tolerated; larger trials would be needed. A useful reminder that not every device story is a success story.

Verified coverage Oct 23, 2026
Source: ScienceDaily (Hypertension, AHA)

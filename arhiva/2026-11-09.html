Medical discoveries

NIH funds dentin-regeneration research

Prof. Anne George at the University of Illinois Chicago received about US$1.2M from the NIH's dental institute (NIDCR) to study the dentin phosphophoryn protein and how it drives autophagy in dental-pulp stem cells. The aim is to regrow dentin and halt cavity progression; the work is still at the laboratory stage, using human pulp stem cells and animal models.

VerifiedNov 3, 2026
Oral Health GroupEurekAlert

Battery-free "self-charged" toothbrush cut plaque in a trial

A study in Science Advances describes a toothbrush with PVDF bristles that generate a small electric charge when they bend, needing no battery. In a 115-person, 3-month trial, plaque scores dropped about 40% versus roughly 15% for a nylon brush, and in vitro it removed over 99% of plaque. These are early results; no commercial product yet.

🧠 4 · Psychology

Adults — a science-backed finding (DSM-5 context)

The medical profile of newly-diagnosed autistic adults has shifted since 2010

A new BMJ Mental Health study of UK and Swedish primary-care records (~34M adults, ~240,000 with an autism diagnosis) found that conditions recorded alongside a new adult autism diagnosis moved away from epilepsy, psychosis and intellectual disability toward ADHD, anxiety and depression. In UK data, prior anxiety among the newly diagnosed rose from ~29% (2010) to ~58% (2023). It is observational and cannot say whether awareness, reduced stigma or changed diagnostic practice is the driver.

VerifiedNov 5, 2026
BMJ GroupEurekAlert

APA publishes a roadmap for the next DSM

The American Psychiatric Association set out its strategy for a future DSM in the American Journal of Psychiatry, signaling a shift from fixed categorical disorders toward dimensional "spectrum of shared symptoms" models that fold in context, transdiagnostic features and biomarkers. Diagnosis remains clinical — there is still no stand-alone biological test — and no firm timeline for a replacement has been set.

Children — the ~6-year-old brain (for Andrei, b. 2020)

Executive function and self-regulation enter a key growth phase

Around age 6, children make large gains in executive function — the "air-traffic-control" skills of working memory, impulse control and flexible attention that let them hold instructions, wait and plan. The prefrontal cortex is still immature, so impulsivity and emotional overwhelm remain normal; these skills are built through practice, routines and supportive relationships. Watch for a growing ability to pause before reacting and to use words instead of acting out, and support it with consistent routines and turn-taking, rule-based games.

Verified · developmental consensus
Harvard Center on the Developing ChildPalomar — Child Development

What to watch for over the coming year

A typical 6-year-old speaks in full 5–7-word sentences with a fast-growing vocabulary, begins basic reading, writing and simple addition/subtraction, forms friendships around shared interests, and gets better at naming feelings (though worries about being liked are common). Flags worth raising with a pediatrician include persistent difficulty with reading/writing/math concepts, marked trouble making or keeping friends, or delayed motor skills — milestones are ranges, so clustering or regression is the trigger to consult.

📚 5 · Education

Education over the next ~20 years vs. classical schooling

OECD: AI will reshape pedagogy and assessment — but tools alone won't

The OECD's Digital Education Outlook 2026 argues that generative AI, used with clear pedagogical intent, can improve learning and foster critical thinking and collaboration — but that isolated tools cannot compensate for misalignment across curriculum, assessment and teacher development. It pushes toward "process-oriented assessment" (grading the learning process, not just a final product AI can now generate), a direct challenge to the classic exam-and-essay model.

VerifiedJan 2026
OECDEPALE (EU)

OECD: shrinking cohorts and aging teachers reshape the school model

Education at a Glance 2026 projects the number of children aged 5–14 across OECD countries will fall about 9% between 2024 and 2033, while one in four secondary teachers is already 55+. The proposed response — using teaching time more productively with digital tools and AI, better workforce planning and redesigned school structures — implies structural change to the traditional age-cohort, one-teacher-per-class model.

VerifiedSep 2026

The World Economic Forum estimates nearly 40% of core job skills will be disrupted within five years, ranking AI/big data, cybersecurity, creative thinking, resilience and curiosity as fastest-rising. At Davos 2026 it launched a "Learning-to-Earning Sandbox" around apprenticeships, micro-credentials and work-integrated degrees — a move away from front-loaded classical schooling toward continuous reskilling. Educating a ~6-year-old (evidence-based)

Positive, attention-based discipline over punishment

The American Academy of Pediatrics' policy on effective discipline recommends teaching children to manage their own behavior, with adult attention as the most powerful tool — reinforcing good behavior by "catching the child being good." It opposes corporal punishment and shaming, and favors clear rules and consistent consequences (e.g., brief time-outs of about one minute per year of age, loss of privileges).

Verified · AAP policy
HealthyChildren (AAP)AAP in Pediatrics (PubMed)

Build skills through play and routine, plus sensible screen limits

Because self-regulation skills are "built, not born," evidence-based guidance for this age favors scaffolded practice — predictable routines, rule-based and pretend play, shared reading, and gradually handing over responsibility — over rote drilling or pressure. Pediatric guidance also recommends firm, consistent limits on recreational screen time so it doesn't crowd out physical play, sleep and family time, paired with daily shared reading.

04 Psychology

Adults — a scientifically grounded finding (DSM-5 context)

Adult ADHD linked to higher risk of late-life cognitive decline

A JAMA Psychiatry cohort of 187,341 adults aged 50+ from the NIH All of Us program found that those with a DSM-5 ADHD diagnosis developed mild cognitive impairment or dementia more often than those without (6.7% vs 3.4%), with a supporting dose-response against ADHD genetic risk. The study is observational — association, not proven causation — and the effect weakened when diagnoses were far apart in time. It published online Oct 7, with major coverage through Oct 13–14.

VerifiedPublished 2026-10-07 · coverage 2026-10-13/14
JAMA Psychiatry·Univ. of Pittsburgh

Depression may be five distinct brain profiles, not one disorder

A University of Helsinki team used magnetoencephalography in 263 adults with major depression plus 75 controls and identified five distinct functional-connectivity profiles — some patients showing stronger, others weaker connectivity than normal. The finding challenges the DSM-5 practice of treating major depression as a single category, though the authors stress the profiles cannot yet guide treatment.

Verified2026-10-08
Helsinki Times·SciTechDaily
Children — what is developing in a ~6-year-old's brain (Andrei, b. 2020)

Cognition shifts from "magical thinking" to logical reasoning

Around age 6 the preschool-era magical thinking fades and the child begins to grasp cause-and-effect, the concept of time, counting and simple addition/subtraction, and sorting by size, color and shape. Over the coming year expect sight-word reading, early writing, and attention stretching to roughly 15 minutes on a single task.

VerifiedCDC / Kaiser guidance (rev. 2024–25)
CDC·Kaiser Permanente

Executive function and the prefrontal cortex are still maturing

At 6, self-regulation is improving but still immature — big emotions can overwhelm the child, who needs adult help to process them. Recent developmental-neuroscience work confirms that prefrontal connectivity underlying inhibitory control, working memory and attention keeps strengthening well beyond age 6, so inconsistent impulse control this year is developmentally normal. Watch for growth in planning and following multi-step instructions.

Friendships and peer acceptance become central

Around 6–7 children pay far more attention to friendships and teamwork and begin to want to be liked; empathy emerges when they are made aware of others' feelings. Watch this year for worries about fitting in, early peer comparison, and the need for coaching on resolving disagreements. A 2025 APA meta-analysis also found a two-way link between screen time and emotional/behavioral problems in young children.

The next ~20 years vs. the classical school

OECD sketches four scenarios for schooling to ~2040

The OECD's scenario work envisions four roughly 20-year futures rather than one: schooling extended and digitalized, education outsourced to market providers, schools becoming community learning hubs, or learning happening anytime/anywhere beyond the traditional school. The message is that the single classical model is one of several possible futures, not a given.

Educating a ~6-year-old (Andrei, b. 2020)

CDC positive-parenting guidance for ages 6–8

Build independence and problem-solving: let him work through peer disagreements himself while coaching when he is upset, offer a few simple choices, assign small chores, and talk regularly about school and friends. Keep discipline clear and consistent, always pairing a "no" with what he should do instead.

Manage screens to support self-regulation and learning

With a 2025 APA meta-analysis showing a two-way loop between screen time and emotional/behavioral problems, the AAP advises focusing on what the child does on screens over rigid limits — its "5 Cs" — while keeping screens out of the bedroom, protecting a screen-free pre-bed period, and co-viewing content. 06 Medicine

Arrowhead's ARO-MAPT cuts brain tau in first human data (Alzheimer's)

Arrowhead reported interim Phase 1/2a results for ARO-MAPT, an RNAi therapeutic targeting the tau gene, showing a mean reduction of up to 73% in cerebrospinal-fluid total tau (max 85%) at Day 105 in healthy volunteers, with a clean early safety profile. The durable knockdown supports infrequent dosing, but effects on cognition and disease progression remain untested in patients.

Lancet Commission names 17 global health threats, two with "extinction potential"

A Lancet Commission launched at the World Health Summit in Berlin quantified 17 interconnected catastrophic threats to human health through 2100 using disease-burden data, classing nuclear war (via global famine) and malicious use of AI as carrying non-zero human-extinction risk this century. It calls for threat-ready health systems, more innovation funding, and an independent annual threat monitor.

"Zombie cells" use PD-L2 to hide from the immune system

Cedars-Sinai researchers reported in Cell Metabolism that senescent ("zombie") cells upregulate the checkpoint protein PD-L2 to evade immune clearance. In mice, blocking or deleting PD-L2 reduced senescent-cell burden and inflammation and improved glucose handling and grip strength; human aged tissues also showed higher PD-L2, pointing to a potential senolytic target. The work is preclinical.

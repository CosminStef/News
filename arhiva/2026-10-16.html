Psychology

Adults — a science-backed finding tied to DSM-5

Five distinct brain "phenotypes" found within one depression diagnosis

Verifiedcoverage 8–9 Oct 2026

A University of Helsinki–led team used resting-state MEG functional connectivity in 263 patients with major depressive disorder and 75 controls, and identified five biologically distinct depression profiles differing in connectivity strength and dominant symptoms. The DSM-5 relevance: it supports the view that MDD as currently defined is not one disease but a heterogeneous group of biological conditions — a possible explanation for why patients respond so differently to the same treatment. The authors call these candidate phenotypes, not yet a clinical tool.

Sources: Nature Mental Health · University of Helsinki

Adults with ADHD show higher cardiorenal risk on starting blood-pressure treatment

Verified2026 (Nature Mental Health)

A Dutch nationwide cohort of 706,414 new users of antihypertensive medication (10,689 with ADHD, none with prior cardiovascular or kidney disease) found adults with ADHD had higher adjusted risk of heart-failure hospitalization (HR 1.46) and stroke (HR 1.19). ADHD is a DSM-5 diagnosis, and the finding reinforces treating it as a condition with physical-health comorbidities. Corroborated by an independent 2026 multinational study.

Sources: Nature Mental Health · The Lancet Primary Care

Children — what develops in a ~6-year-old's brain (Andrei, b. 2020)

The points below are evidence-based developmental references (CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early.", AAP, WHO), not breaking news. CDC milestones describe what ≥75% of children can do by an age — guidelines, not pass/fail tests.

Self-regulation and more complex emotions come online

Verifiedreference (CDC)

Around 5–6 the brain's executive-function and self-regulation systems advance: the child follows rules and takes turns, does simple chores, and begins feeling more complex emotions (fairness, guilt, embarrassment). Watch for a growing — but still adult-supported — ability to manage frustration, and for peers and teachers starting to supplement parents as social reference points.

Source: CDC milestones (5 yr)

From "magical thinking" to cause-and-effect, and early literacy/numeracy

Verifiedreference (CDC)

Preschool "magical thinking" fades as cause-and-effect reasoning emerges; children count past 10, grasp simple addition/subtraction, begin telling time, write letters (e.g. their name), and tell stories with at least two events. Over the coming year, watch for a lengthening attention span and the move into early reading and writing that formal schooling builds on.

Source: CDC checklist (5 yr)

Motor control sharpens; first permanent teeth arrive

Verifiedreference (CDC / Kaiser)

Gross and fine motor control improve: hopping on one foot, riding a two-wheeled bike, more accurate throwing and catching, and finer hand control for scissors, tying shoes and writing. Around 6, first molars appear and baby teeth start to be lost; expect roughly ~6 cm growth and ~3 kg gain over the year.

Sources: CDC · Kaiser Permanente

If something seems off, screen — don't wait

Verifiedreference (CDC)

Milestone checklists are not a substitute for formal developmental screening. If a child isn't meeting expected milestones, or loses skills, CDC guidance is to raise it with the pediatrician and ask about a developmental screening. The free CDC Milestone Tracker app helps monitor progress.

Source: CDC 5-year checklist

Education

Education over the next ~20 years vs. classical schooling

OECD — four scenarios for schooling to ~2040

VerifiedOECD report

The OECD's "Back to the Future of Education" lays out four futures rather than one forecast: schooling expanded and modernized; education outsourced to a diverse market; schools persisting but becoming hubs of experimentation; and "learning as you go," where formal schooling breaks down and society educates directly. The through-line is that today's mass-schooling model is only one of several plausible paths.

Source: OECD (PDF)

VerifiedWEF publication

The WEF contrasts a baseline of modernizing and extending today's schooling with a "transformed" scenario in which AI and related technology allow knowledge, skills and attitudes to be assessed and certified almost instantly — loosening the link between learning and the traditional classroom and credential.

Source: World Economic Forum

How to educate a child of Andrei's age (~5–6)

Play is a learning engine, not a break from it

Verifiedreference (AAP)

The AAP "Power of Play" clinical report concludes that developmentally appropriate, often child-led play builds social-emotional, cognitive, language and self-regulation skills, strengthens executive function and brain structure, and buffers stress through attuned parent-child interaction. Practical takeaway: protect unstructured and pretend play — it is doing cognitive work.

Source: AAP clinical report (Pediatrics)

Responsive, warm caregiving plus everyday learning

Verifiedreference (WHO)

WHO's early-childhood-development guideline centers "responsive caregiving" — noticing, understanding and promptly responding to a child's signals — combined with everyday learning activities. At 5–6 that means conversation-rich, back-and-forth interaction and shared activities rather than passive instruction, plus daily shared reading and embedding counting/early math into play and chores.

Source: WHO guideline

Consistent routines, chores and clear rules build self-regulation

Verifiedreference (CDC)

Because 6-year-olds are developing rule-following, turn-taking and responsibility, CDC-aligned positive-parenting guidance recommends simple household chores, consistent limits and praise for cooperation — scaffolding the executive-function and emotional-regulation skills still maturing this year.

Source: CDC positive parenting / milestones

Medicine

Within the strict 24–72h window only Item 1 qualifies; Items 2–4 are 7–9 days old but are the best-verified medical firsts/approvals of this cycle, with ages stated.

Chemo-free regimen beats standard care in adult Ph+ ALL (NEJM)

Verified14 Oct 2026

A randomized phase 3 trial (236 adults) found that a chemotherapy-free frontline regimen of ponatinib plus the bispecific antibody blinatumomab substantially improved event-free survival over standard imatinib-plus-chemotherapy in Philadelphia-chromosome-positive acute lymphoblastic leukemia (reported ~24-month EFS roughly 86% vs 67%). The result supports a shift toward targeted, chemo-sparing first-line treatment. Secondary figures cited; the NEJM full text is authoritative.

Sources: NEJM · ASCO Post

FDA approves pritelivir — first new-mechanism HSV antiviral in ~30 years

Verified9 Oct 2026 (~7 days old)

The FDA approved pritelivir (HOVILPRI), an oral helicase-primase inhibitor, for mucocutaneous herpes simplex lesions in immunocompromised adults whose infection is refractory to acyclovir/valacyclovir/famciclovir — the first antiviral with a novel mechanism against HSV in nearly three decades. In the phase 3 PRIOH-1 trial (101 adults), complete lesion healing by day 28 reached 63% vs 34% for investigator's choice.

Sources: Contagion Live · Pharmacy Times

FDA approves first adjuvant immunotherapy for stage III dMMR colon cancer

Verified8 Oct 2026 (~8 days old)

The FDA approved atezolizumab (Tecentriq) with mFOLFOX6 chemotherapy as adjuvant (post-surgery) treatment for resected stage III mismatch-repair-deficient colon cancer — the first immunotherapy in this curative-intent setting. In the phase 3 ATOMIC trial (~712 patients) the combination roughly halved recurrence/death risk (disease-free survival HR 0.50).

Sources: FDA · Roche

Optogenetic therapy partially restores vision in advanced retinitis pigmentosa (NEJM)

Verified7 Oct 2026 (~9 days old)

An open-label safety study in 10 blind patients with advanced retinitis pigmentosa used a single intravitreal injection of a viral vector delivering the light-sensitive protein ChrimsonR into surviving retinal ganglion cells, paired with light-projecting goggles. The therapy was safe and partially restored aspects of visual function, extending the group's 2021 first-in-human proof of concept. Evidence is preliminary given the small, uncontrolled cohort.

Sources: NEJM · STAT News

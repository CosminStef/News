4. Psychology

Adults (DSM-5 linked)

Overlapping fear/safety brain maps may explain PTSD and anxiety

In mice, researchers found that under threat the hippocampus does not sharpen the line between "danger" and "safety" memories as assumed — the neural maps overlap, which speeds threat recall at the cost of precision. The team frames anxiety and PTSD (DSM-5 trauma and anxiety categories) as a possibly overactive version of a normal survival mechanism rather than a broken system.

Verified2026-10ReportU. Toronto

Cutting social media use linked to lower depression and anxiety

A JAMA Network Open cohort of young adults found a one-week self-selected reduction in social-media use was associated with meaningfully lower depression, anxiety and insomnia (re-circulated 18 November). The design is observational and self-selected — screen time did not actually fall — so causality is not established. Maps to DSM-5 depressive and anxiety disorders.

Partial2025-11-03PubMedNPR

Child (~6-year-old; born 2020)

What develops around age six — and what to watch for

Near age six the brain shifts from preschool "magical thinking" toward cause-and-effect reasoning: attention lengthens to roughly 15–30 minutes, time concepts and early reading, writing and simple arithmetic emerge, and peer approval and friendships gain weight. Watch-list for the coming year: loss of previously acquired skills, persistent inability to tell real from pretend, and undetected vision problems (squinting, eye-rubbing, trouble reading at distance) — flag any to a paediatrician, using the CDC milestone checklist as the reference.

Educating a ~6-year-old (born 2020)

Explicit phonics plus guided play

For this age the strongest evidence supports structured, adult-guided play that targets specific literacy skills — letter recognition, sounding out blends, oral language — layered on top of explicit, systematic phonics, rather than free play alone. A high-security UK EEF trial found play-based maths reasoning improved attainment in 6–7-year-olds, while a review cautioned that guided play's literacy benefits are weaker, so phonics must stay central. The evidence is genuinely mixed.

6. Medicine

Retatrutide delivers up to ~25–30% weight loss in Phase 3

Eli Lilly's triple-agonist retatrutide reported average weight loss ranging from about 17.6% to 25.0% across doses at 80 weeks versus 3.9% on placebo, with an extension reaching roughly 30%, moving the once-weekly injectable toward FDA filing. A companion trial also showed osteoarthritis-pain relief. Topline came in May and was re-covered on 8 November; the full peer-reviewed paper is not yet confirmed.

Verified2026-11-08Pharma JournalHealthline

Wireless "ECHO" implant lowers blood sugar without insulin (preclinical)

An ETH Zurich implant converts excess glucose into gluconate (cleared in urine) via a metal-on-graphite catalyst, switched on and off by a continuous glucose monitor linked to a phone — no insulin, drugs or living cells. In diabetic mice, blood sugar normalised within 60–90 minutes and treated animals lost up to ~10% body weight over six weeks with no detectable hypoglycaemia. Not yet tested in humans.

Verified2026-10-22Science NewsMedical Dialogues

FDA approves first precision cell therapy for matched-donor transplant

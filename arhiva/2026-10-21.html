4Psychology

Adults — new finding (DSM-5 related)

Three brain pathways predict lasting relief from DBS in resistant depression

Mount Sinai researchers analysed the largest multi-cohort group yet of patients given subcallosal cingulate deep brain stimulation for treatment-resistant major depression (a DSM-5 presentation). Sustained improvement was consistently linked to stimulating three white-matter tracts — the cingulum bundle, forceps minor and medial uncinate/ventral amygdalofugal pathway — supporting connectome-guided electrode targeting now in trials. These are associations from observational cohorts, and the therapy remains investigational.

Children — a ~6-year-old's brain (Andrei, b. 2020)

Established developmental science (not breaking news). Around age six children move out of preschool "magical thinking" toward cause-and-effect reasoning and a grasp of time; language and literacy accelerate sharply, making this the year to watch reading fluency take off. Socially, parents remain the main source of comfort but teachers and friends grow in importance, while some earlier fears persist. The practical watch-points for the coming year: emerging logical reasoning, reading and writing progress, widening peer relationships and early self-regulation — raise any concerns with a pediatrician early. Authoritative references: CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early," AAP healthychildren.org, and Columbia Doctors milestones.

6Medicine

New discoveries & firsts

Alzheimer's sleep loss tied to overactive microglia, not plaques

In Alzheimer's-model mice, temporarily removing about 87% of brain microglia restored more than two hours of daily sleep without changing amyloid plaque burden, implicating neuroinflammation rather than plaques in sleep disruption. The work is in mice only; any future therapy would aim to calm, not delete, microglia. Originally published in July, it was heavily re-covered this week.

Medical discoveries & research

Dental antimicrobial K21 primes immune cells to kill bacteria

A Rutgers-co-led study in Life Science Alliance reported that K21 — a quaternary ammonium silane already used in an FDA-cleared cavity cleanser — pre-conditions human macrophages to kill Enterococcus faecalis more effectively, an effect that persisted even after the compound was rinsed away before bacterial exposure. Gene analysis pointed to shifts in cellular energy use and mitochondrial housekeeping, and the authors propose a future topical formulation for hard-to-heal diabetic wounds, though wound healing itself was not tested.

Partial 18 Nov 2026 Sources: News-Medical, Journal Arta

Psychology

Adults — new, science-backed (relative to DSM-5)

Intolerance of uncertainty is rising in young adults, tracking anxiety

A temporal meta-analysis from Columbia (Mailman) and Boston University, published in JAMA Psychiatry, found that "intolerance of uncertainty" — a cognitive trait linked to anxiety and mood disorders in DSM-5 — rose steadily among US and Canadian college-age adults from 1992–2023, with the steepest climb from about 2012 (roughly three times faster than in prior decades). The authors treat it as an intermediary cognitive process rather than a proven root cause and suggest reducing it could help curb rising anxiety symptoms.

Verified 18 Nov 2026 Sources: Columbia, News-Medical
Context (emerging, not settled): a transdiagnostic neuroscience study of 134 participants across 11 diagnostic groups reported a shared brain pattern — reduced flexibility shifting from rest into effortful cognitive control — across conditions from depression to schizophrenia (PsyPost, early Nov).
Children — the brain of a ~6-year-old (Andrei, b. 2020)

What is developing now, and what to watch over the coming year

At six, the brain is in rapid growth with active synaptic pruning and increasing myelination, and a maturing prefrontal cortex — which shows up as big gains in self-regulation and reasoning (pediatric sources call these the year's largest growth areas). Language is largely fluent and grammatical; the child can state their age, retell a favourite story and grasp safety rules, while first permanent molars erupt and baby teeth begin to fall out.

For age 6–7, watch for continued growth in early literacy and number sense, longer attention span and following multi-step instructions, and more cooperative friendships — while preschool-type fears (monsters, large animals, being alone) remain normal. Because milestones vary widely, authorities advise raising concerns at well-child visits if a child is markedly behind peers in speech clarity, cannot follow simple rules, shows no interest in other children, or regresses. CDC's formal milestone checklists run through age 5, so the 5-year list is the nearest official anchor.

Verified (evergreen) Current as of 2026 Sources: Kaiser Permanente, ColumbiaDoctors

Education

The next 20 years vs. classical schooling

OECD's four scenarios for schooling to 2040

The OECD's "Back to the Future of Education" framework sketches four futures against today's mass-schooling model: schooling extended (the current model intensifies), education outsourced (a surge of private/market learning), schools as learning hubs (schools become open community centres), and learn-as-you-go (school-based learning dissolves into continuous, technology-mediated learning). The OECD stresses these are tools for strategic dialogue, not predictions, and argues education must stay a public good across all four.

Verified Evergreen reference Sources: OECD, WEF

2026 forecasts: AI as infrastructure, skills over seat time, a widening access gap

Outlook reports (HolonIQ, Marzano Research, Forbes analysis) converge on several directions relative to classical schooling: AI moving from pilots to system-level infrastructure; growing emphasis on measurable competencies and industry micro-credentials alongside or instead of degrees; and a widening "AI access gap" where some families lack devices or software. These are analyst and vendor forecasts, not experimental findings.

Partial 2026 reports Sources: HolonIQ, Marzano Research
Educating a child Andrei's age (~5–6)

Guided play plus clear learning goals

The strongest current evidence favours "guided play" — adult-scaffolded play with a defined learning objective — over both pure direct instruction and unstructured free play for early skills such as math and spatial tasks. The UK Education Endowment Foundation estimates play-based approaches add roughly four months of progress but rates the evidence security as low, advising that any approach be adopted and then evaluated. The consistent practical takeaway: adults are central, and play works best when it is purposeful and goal-directed.

Partial Evergreen · current Sources: EEF, Edutopia

Medicine

Leukaemia traced to a first mutation acquired 50+ years before diagnosis

A Nature study (Cambridge / Sanger / UCL) used serially stored blood samples as a "fossil record" to reconstruct how acute myeloid leukaemia develops, finding the first cancer-driving mutation was acquired before age 20 in about two-thirds of cases — more than 50 years before diagnosis — followed by slow clonal growth. The cohort was small and all female; the authors say it raises the prospect of pre-emptive treatment in high-risk people.

Verified 11 Nov 2026 Sources: UCL, Sanger

FDA approves durvalumab + enfortumab vedotin for muscle-invasive bladder cancer

The FDA approved Imfinzi (durvalumab) plus Padcev (enfortumab vedotin) before radical cystectomy, followed by durvalumab after surgery, for muscle-invasive bladder cancer patients ineligible for or refusing cisplatin — based on the phase 3 VOLGA trial (695 patients), which met its event-free-survival endpoint. It expands a non-cisplatin perioperative option; overall-survival follow-up is ongoing.

Verified 13 Nov 2026 Sources: FDA, Pharmacy Times

Lp(a)-lowering drug pelacarsen cuts the marker 78% — but not cardiac events

The phase 3 Lp(a) HORIZON trial found pelacarsen reduced lipoprotein(a) by 78% in adults with elevated Lp(a) and established cardiovascular disease, yet showed no reduction in the composite of nonfatal heart attack, stroke or urgent revascularisation — an unexpected neutral outcome for a long-pursued risk factor. It was presented at AHA Scientific Sessions 2026 and published in the NEJM.

Verified 7 Nov 2026 Sources: Cleveland Clinic, AHA Newsroom

AI flags bladder cancer from health records up to 5 years early

University of Plymouth researchers built PRECISE-AGZ, a machine-learning model mining electronic health records that reportedly identified 85% of patients who went on to develop bladder cancer, with 91% accuracy in cancer-free people, performing best within 12 months but with signals up to 5 years earlier — outperforming current NHS referral criteria. It is a retrospective study, not yet a validated screening programme, and some coverage disputes which figures were formally released.

Partial November 2026 Sources: Univ. of Plymouth, SciTechDaily

1Dental

Medical / scientific discoveries

Scaffold-free "Bio3D" graft regenerates periodontal tissue in preclinical model

Researchers bioprinted a scaffold-free graft built from human mesenchymal stromal cells and their own extracellular matrix. In critical-size periodontal defects in immunodeficient rats it regenerated cementum, periodontal ligament and alveolar bone, with the effect linked to YAP/TAZ signalling. This is an animal-model study with no human clinical data yet.

Partial 23 Nov 2026 Source: npj Regenerative Medicine

4Psychology

Adults — evidence-based finding (DSM-5 relevant)

Treating anhedonia ("Positive Affect Treatment") outperforms standard therapy

A randomized controlled trial (SMU/UCLA; Meuret & Craske) found that Positive Affect Treatment — which rebuilds the capacity to feel pleasure, reward and purpose — produced greater improvement in overall clinical status than conventional therapy targeting negative affect, with the advantage holding at 1-month follow-up. It directly targets anhedonia, a core DSM-5 criterion for Major Depressive Disorder that affects ~90% of people with depression and predicts a worse course. Mechanism involves measured modulation of brain reward and threat processing.

Verified Published 24 Apr 2026 (latest credible RCT) Sources: SMU · Neuroscience News · DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.7403
No fully novel adult study dated exactly 22–24 Nov 2026 could be verified to publication standard; the above is the most recent DSM-5-relevant RCT. Secondary (Partial): a Dutch cohort reports anxiety/depression episodes lasting longer today than a decade ago.

Children — brain development at ~6 (Andrei, b. 2020)

What develops in a 6-year-old's brain this year

Around age six the prefrontal cortex matures through synaptic pruning (removing excess connections), underpinning executive functions — attention, working memory and impulse inhibition. Reading circuitry becomes more efficient mainly through better connectivity rather than shrinking, and the ability to hear and understand rhyme is a key predictor of reading readiness. Typical markers: 5–7-word sentences, decoding unfamiliar words, a ~10,000-word vocabulary, growing self-control; residual early-childhood fears remain normal.

Verified (developmental science) Sources: APA · AAP milestones

What to watch for in the coming year

Flags worth raising with the pediatrician: persistent difficulty learning to read or sound out words, not meeting language/cognitive milestones, marked struggle in specific school subjects, or very short/erratic attention beyond age norms. Note that CDC's validated milestone checklists end at age 5, so for age six rely on AAP, APA and the pediatric well-visit; any concern should prompt a standardized developmental screen.

Verified (reference) Sources: CDC Act Early · AAP · APA

5Education

Next ~20 years vs. classical schooling

Major bodies forecast a shift toward competency-based learning — with caveats on AI

Rather than a single "AI replaces school" prediction, major institutions publish scenarios. The OECD frames the future via its "Four Scenarios for the Future of Schooling" and Future of Education & Skills 2030/2040, pointing toward project- and competency-based learning; its Digital Education Outlook 2026 warns that gen-AI benefits often shrink or reverse when the AI is removed (e.g. in exams) and that pervasive devices tend to worsen distraction. WEF's Future of Jobs projects ~23% of jobs change by 2030, prizing problem-solving, critical thinking, creativity and collaboration. Brookings (2026) sees upside in personalized pathways and AI tutoring but warns broad general-purpose AI use can diminish learning and fray human/relational bonds.

Verified 2025–2026 reports Sources: OECD · WEF · Brookings

Educating a ~6-year-old (evidence-based)

Play- and relationship-rich learning, limited general-purpose AI

Evidence favours play- and project-based, relationship-rich learning over heavy screen/device drilling at this age, building reading readiness through phonological/rhyming play and protecting human interaction over AI substitution. Outdoor/green time matters: a University of Bristol study reported children who visit parks/playgrounds at least weekly show better mental health and social development. Keep attention tasks short — a six-year-old's sustained attention is roughly 6–20 minutes.

Verified principles Bristol study: Partial Bristol reported 10 Nov 2026 Sources: APA · Brookings (above) · Bristol (single primary source)

6Medicine

New discoveries & firsts

First drug for the neurological symptoms of Gaucher disease type 3

The FDA approved Sanofi's venglustat (brand Infilbray), an oral, brain-penetrant glucosylceramide-synthase inhibitor — the first therapy ever cleared specifically for the neurological manifestations of type 3 Gaucher disease, which prior enzyme therapies cannot reach. Approval rests on the Phase 3 LEAP2MONO trial showing significant improvement in a combined ataxia + cognition measure at week 52. The FDA flagged serious psychiatric risks including suicidality.

Verified 18 Nov 2026 Sources: NeurologyLive · BioPharma Dive

Daraxonrasib roughly doubles survival in previously-treated metastatic pancreatic cancer

The Phase 3 RASolute 302 trial (500 patients) showed the oral pan-RAS(ON) inhibitor daraxonrasib raised median overall survival to 13.2 months vs 6.6–6.7 months with chemotherapy (~60% lower risk of death), and was far better tolerated. The European Commission granted approval on 16 Nov 2026 — a notable advance in a cancer long resistant to targeted therapy.

Verified NEJM 31 May 2026; EU approval 16 Nov 2026 Sources: NEJM/PubMed · UCLA

Giredestrant approved for ESR1-mutated advanced breast cancer

The FDA approved oral giredestrant (Wilserdly) plus everolimus for ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated advanced breast cancer that progressed on prior endocrine therapy, based on the Phase 3 evERA trial. A separate, broader early-stage (adjuvant) application remains pending with an FDA target date of 30 Nov 2026; overall-survival benefit is not yet established.

Verified 16 Nov 2026 Sources: ASCO Post · AJMC

Large analyses: GLP-1 drugs around conception show no clearly detectable rise in major risks

Large 2026 cohort and pooled analyses (a Danish 750,000-pregnancy study; a Harvard-led 2.1M-pregnancy study) found no clearly detectable increase in miscarriage, stillbirth, congenital anomalies or most obstetric outcomes after periconceptional GLP-1 exposure. The data are observational; authors stress this is not proof of safety in pregnancy, and the Danish study did find higher preterm-birth risk confined to women treated for diabetes. Guidance still advises stopping before a planned pregnancy.

Partial (observational) Primary studies Mar 2026; re-reported ~11–13 Nov 2026 Sources: Harvard Chan · City St George's

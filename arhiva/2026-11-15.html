4.Psychology

Adults — recent findings

Drug ads can shift AI chatbot treatment recommendations

A Mount Sinai team tested 12 large AI models on physician-written clinical cases. Inserting a single clearly-labeled drug advertisement raised selection of the advertised drug from about 35% to 47.6% of responses — often among options that were all clinically appropriate — without lowering overall accuracy. It bears on AI use in clinical and mental-health decision support, though it is not a DSM-5 diagnostic study.

VerifiedNov 11, 2026 (The Lancet Digital Health)

Sources: News-Medical · Medical Economics

Body awareness (interoception) buffers the health toll of political stress

In a longitudinal US sample spanning the 2024 election, adults who more accurately sensed and trusted internal bodily signals reported fewer negative health effects from political stress while staying civically engaged. Interoception was measured by self-report rather than physiological markers, a methodological limit; there is no direct DSM-5 link, but it relates to stress and anxiety mechanisms.

Children — what develops in a ~6-year-old (Andrei, b. 2020)
CDC's official milestone checklists run birth-to-5, so age-6 guidance below draws on the CDC 5-year checklist plus AAP/HealthyChildren and developmental-psychology consensus. These are evergreen references, not 24h news.

Executive function & attention (prefrontal cortex)

Around 5–6, rapid prefrontal-cortex maturation drives gains in attention span, impulse control, working memory and rule-following. Over the coming year expect longer focus and better self-regulation. Watch for persistent inability to sit for a short task, follow 2–3 step instructions, or control impulses relative to peers — worth flagging to the pediatrician for developmental screening.

VerifiedCDC · AAP (evergreen)

Sources: CDC Act Early · HealthyChildren (AAP)

Language explosion & emergent literacy

By 6, most children speak in grammatically correct full sentences, tell a simple story, write some letters and numbers and their name, and begin sounding out simple words. In the coming year watch for the start of true reading; difficulty hearing or segmenting sounds in words (phonological awareness), or no interest in letters, can be early markers of reading difficulty.

VerifiedCDC · PBS (evergreen)

Sources: CDC · PBS Parents

Social-emotional growth: friendships & theory of mind

This is the fastest-growing area at 6 — children form real friendships, want to please peers, understand others' feelings, and manage emotions better. Typical fears (the dark, monsters, large animals) remain normal. Over the year watch for ongoing struggles making or keeping friends, inability to calm after upsets, or extreme/persistent fears or aggression out of step with peers.

VerifiedCDC/AAP · Sesame Workshop (evergreen)

Sources: CDC · Sesame Workshop

How to educate a ~6-year-old (evidence-based)

Explicit, systematic phonics is the core of early reading

Decades of "science of reading" research support structured, systematic phonics — teaching letter-sounds (not just letter names) in a planned sequence — as the most effective route to reading. For a 6-year-old, introduce a few new sounds each week while reviewing prior ones, paired with decodable reading practice.

VerifiedScience-of-reading consensus (evergreen)

Sources: PBS Parents · Tools of the Mind

Guided play beats pure direct instruction at this age

Delivering literacy and math goals through guided play — adult-designed activities (blocks, drawing, dramatic play) with a clear learning target — improves oral language, letter recognition and decoding more than rote drilling. A practical rule: set one specific skill goal per game rather than leaving play entirely unstructured.

6.Medicine

First multicenter CAR-T trial for stiff person syndrome shows positive results

A nonrandomized multicenter trial (with Mayo Clinic as one of three sites) of miv-cel, a CD19 CAR-T therapy, treated 26 adults with treatment-refractory stiff person syndrome, a rare autoimmune neurologic disorder. The primary endpoint — a timed 25-foot walk at week 16 — was met with roughly 45.6% median improvement, sustained at week 24; most adverse events were low-grade. Durability remains unproven and follow-up is ongoing.

VerifiedAnnounced ~Nov 9–11, 2026 (JAMA Neurology)

Sources: News-Medical · EurekAlert · Newswise

Viral infection may leave hidden, lasting lung/immune damage

Research indicates respiratory viral RNA and protein can persist for weeks inside lung immune cells after apparent recovery in mice, driving chronic inflammation and abnormal lung-cell development; clearing the infected cells reduced damage.

Psychology

Adults — a science-backed finding (DSM-5 lens)

Legal online sports betting linked to more depression & anxiety treatment

14 Oct 2026Verified

A quasi-experimental study in JAMA Psychiatry compared US states that legalised online sports betting (2018–2022) with those that did not, using commercial-insurance claims (2016–2023). Legalisation was associated with ~5–7% relative increases in outpatient depression and anxiety diagnoses and in antidepressant/anxiolytic prescriptions, with no rise in substance-use diagnoses or ER/inpatient visits. It is an association — the data cannot confirm which individuals actually gambled.

DSM-5 link: maps to Depressive Disorders (MDD) and Anxiety Disorders; the underlying behaviour relates to Gambling Disorder, the only behavioural addiction in DSM-5.

Sources: JAMA Psychiatry · Johns Hopkins · Medscape

Children — the brain of a ~6-year-old (Andrei, b. 2020)

What develops around age 6 — and what to watch this coming year

reference (AAP 2018 + milestone science)Partial

Around age 6 the prefrontal cortex grows rapidly, driving executive function — working memory, impulse control, focus and flexible thinking — while synaptic pruning and myelination make thinking faster and more organised (the classic "5-to-7 shift"). Cognitively a child moves from learning-to-read toward decoding simple words, grasps basic number and time concepts, and develops stronger theory of mind; socially they play cooperatively and want to follow rules, but still carry preschool fears and limited emotional regulation. The AAP stresses that play — including unstructured and guided "playful learning" — is brain-building and buffers stress.

Watch for this year: persistent trouble recognising letters/sounds or no interest in print; delays across several skills at once; speech that's hard to understand; impulsivity or inattention far beyond age norms; extreme separation anxiety, persistent sadness/irritability or disruptive aggression; or loss of previously acquired skills (regression) — any of these warrant a pediatrician check. (CDC milestone checklists formally run to age 5, so the 5-year checklist is the nearest CDC reference for a child just turning 6.)

Sources: AAP "Power of Play" (Yogman et al., Pediatrics 2018) · Kaiser Permanente — milestones for 6-year-olds

Education

Education over the next ~20 years vs. the classic school

OECD's four scenarios for schooling to 2040

2020, updated 2025Verified

The OECD offers not a single forecast but four scenarios to 2040: Schooling Extended (traditional schools persist, scaled up with digital tools); Education Outsourced (learning fragments across private/online providers, weakening the school's monopoly); Schools as Learning Hubs (schools remain but open up and network with communities); and Learn-as-you-go (formal structures largely dissolve into AI-driven, on-demand learning). The 2025 "Trends Shaping Education" update stress-tests these with continuation/disruption/collapse archetypes. Across all four, the contrast with classic schooling is less reliance on fixed age-grade classrooms, more AI personalisation, blurred school boundaries and lifelong rather than front-loaded learning — deliberately "possible futures," not predictions.

Sources: OECD — Back to the Future of Education · OECD — Trends Shaping Education 2025

How to educate a ~6-year-old (Andrei, b. 2020)

Evidence-based guidance: read daily, let play lead, talk a lot

reference (AAP + literacy research)Partial

Evidence points to combining: (a) daily shared/dialogic reading — the AAP recommends shared reading through at least kindergarten, favouring print over screens; (b) learning through play and "guided play" rather than heavy direct instruction, which the AAP's "Power of Play" shows builds executive function and buffers stress; (c) at age 6, supporting the move from learning-to-read toward decoding (letter sounds plus context), with word games and conversation that grow vocabulary (a strong predictor of later reading); and (d) limited recreational screen time in favour of hands-on, social activity. Practical takeaway: read together daily, let play lead with light adult guidance, talk a lot, keep learning low-pressure, and protect sleep and unstructured time.

Sources: AAP "Power of Play" · PBS Parents — reading at age 6

New discoveries & firsts (science-backed)

Sacituzumab tirumotecan beats chemo in endometrial cancer (Phase 3)

26 Oct 2026Verified

The Phase 3 TroFuse-005 trial (776 patients, 31 countries) found the TROP2-directed antibody-drug conjugate sacituzumab tirumotecan cut the risk of death by ~32% and of progression-or-death by ~41% versus physician's-choice chemotherapy in previously treated advanced/recurrent endometrial cancer. Results were presented at ESMO 2026 (Madrid) and published simultaneously in The Lancet; an FDA decision is expected by June 2027.

Sources: CancerNetwork · ecancer (ESMO) · NYU Langone

First remote robotic surgery between two US hospitals

26 Oct 2026Partial

Dr. Vipul Patel, operating from AdventHealth Celebration in Florida, remotely controlled a surgical robot to remove the prostate of a 66-year-old patient about 35 miles away in Minneola — described as the first such telesurgery between two US hospitals. It is part of an FDA-authorised clinical research study. Coverage traces largely to a single ABC exclusive plus AdventHealth's own release, so independent confirmation is limited.

Sources: GMA / ABC News · AdventHealth

Ivonescimab + chemo improves survival in first-line biliary tract cancer

25 Oct 2026Verified

In the Phase 3 HARMONi-GI1 trial (682 patients, China), the bispecific antibody ivonescimab plus gemcitabine/cisplatin produced median overall survival of 14.9 vs 12.1 months (HR 0.75) and median PFS of 8.4 vs 5.8 months (HR 0.61) versus durvalumab plus chemotherapy. Presented as a late-breaker at ESMO 2026 and framed as a potential new first-line standard — with the caveat that it was a single-region, China-only population.

Sources: OncLive · CancerNetwork

Dental medical discoveries

TiO₂ nanotubes strengthen soft denture-liner bonding

A Journal of Prosthetic Dentistry study (Mutlu et al.) found that adding functionalised titanium-dioxide nanotubes to an interim soft denture liner raised tensile bond strength without a meaningful short-term loss of hardness, and added an antifungal benefit. The underlying paper was published online in September; the lay write-up is the recent item, not a brand-new study.

4Psychology

Adults — new finding (DSM-5 relevant)

Low psychosocial status linked to ~4× higher long-term depression risk

A prospective UK Biobank cohort of 336,922 middle-aged adults with no baseline depression built an 18-factor "psychosocial status" score. Those in the lowest tier had an adjusted hazard ratio of ~4.36 for developing clinically recorded depression (DSM-5 Major Depressive Disorder) versus the highest tier, with low income, recent depressive symptoms and loneliness the strongest drivers. It is observational — association, not causation — and Biobank volunteers are healthier than the general population.

Verifiedreported ~13–23 Nov 2026
Sources: Epidemiology & Psychiatric Sciences · Mental Health Daily

Children — Andrei (born 2020, ~6 years old)

At 6, Andrei sits at the transition from early into middle childhood (the start of Piaget's concrete-operational stage). Current developmental-science consensus for this age:

  • Cognitive: rapid growth in logical reasoning, counting, early reading/writing, sense of time and sequence, and sustained attention; can follow multi-step instructions; theory of mind consolidating.
  • Emotional: beginning self-regulation (managing frustration with less adult help, still uneven); residual preschool fears fade; growing sensitivity to fairness and to others' opinions.
  • Social: strong drive toward peer friendships and cooperative, rule-based play; self-esteem starts tying to competence and peer acceptance — the beginning of social comparison.
  • Motor: refining fine motor (writing, scissors, tying laces, buttoning) and gross motor (hopping, skipping, catching, cycling); dresses independently.

What to watch in the coming year: the CDC's official milestone checklists stop at age 5, so the 5-year checklist plus pediatric guidance is the reference. Prompts for a pediatric conversation (screening, not diagnosis): persistent trouble following simple instructions, no interest in playing with other children, extreme separation difficulty, loss of previously acquired skills, inability to focus for several minutes, or marked speech/fine-motor/early-literacy delays.

Sources: CDC — Act Early · AAP Bright Futures (6-year visit)

Education over the next ~20 years vs classical schooling

AI as tutoring at scale — augmentation, not replacement (WEF)

The World Economic Forum's Shaping the Future of Learning work argues AI can dynamically adjust content, pace and difficulty per learner — emulating one-to-one tutoring at scale — while offloading administrative tasks and partly easing teacher shortages. The framing is augmentation of teachers rather than replacement of schools.

Analysis — reputable source
Source: WEF (PDF)

The skills mix shifts sharply by 2030 (WEF Future of Jobs)

WEF projects ~170M new roles and ~92M displaced by 2030 (net +78M), with ~39% of workers' core skills expected to change. Analytical thinking, resilience, flexibility and AI/tech literacy rise, while routine reading, writing and basic numeracy fall in relative demand — implying classical, rote-weighted curricula need rebalancing toward higher-order, adaptive competencies.

Analysis — reputable source
Source: WEF Future of Jobs 2025

Generative-AI tutors change instruction, with caveats (OECD)

The OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 notes generative AI can hold flexible, adaptive dialogue unlike older rule-based tutors, but explicitly does not foresee AI replacing schools or teachers, flagging risks to learner autonomy and the need for universal AI literacy.

Analysis — reputable source
Source: OECD Trends Shaping Education

How to educate a child of Andrei's age (~6)

  • Discipline that teaches, not punishes: consistent, reasonable limits and calm, predictable consequences; the AAP frames discipline as teaching and protecting. Coach him to step away and self-soothe when angry — this builds the self-regulation developing now.
  • Routines and structure: predictable daily routines (sleep, meals, homework, play) give 6-year-olds security and support attention and behaviour.
  • Read together and independently: shared reading supports language, literacy and social-emotional development; pair read-alouds with encouraging his own early reading.
  • Quality-focused media (AAP "5 C's"): Content, Context, Calm, Crowding-out, Communication — keep screens from displacing sleep, activity, play and family time; a written family media plan helps.
  • Play, movement, social skills: prioritise active free play and peer interaction over early academic pressure.
Sources: AAP Bright Futures · HealthyChildren (AAP)

6Medicine

Pan-RAS "molecular glue" shows early efficacy in RAS-mutant cancers

Erasca's ERAS-0015, a pan-RAS molecular-glue degrader, reported first-in-human Phase 1 data (AURORAS-1) at the EORTC-NCI-AACR Symposium in Barcelona. Confirmed tumour shrinkage occurred in 11/25 (44%) previously treated metastatic pancreatic-cancer patients at the 32 mg dose, and ~58–60% in a small RAS-mutant lung-cancer cohort, described as generally well tolerated (one treatment-related death noted). Cohorts are small with limited follow-up; a Phase 3 pancreatic trial is planned for H1 2027.

Verified~20–22 Nov 2026
Sources: Mirage News · CP24

Blood test to detect CTE in living people

A Boston University–Banner Health team reported a blood tau biomarker significantly elevated in people with advanced chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) — previously diagnosable only at autopsy — validated in 21 former football players who gave blood while alive and later donated their brains. A second marker helped distinguish CTE from Alzheimer's. It detects mainly advanced-stage disease, is not FDA-approved, and is not yet clinically usable (researchers estimate ~5 years to possible approval).

Verified~19–20 Nov 2026
Sources: WBUR · CBS News

AI tool reported to flag bladder cancer up to 5 years early

A reported AI model analysing records of ~70,000 patients is said to identify ~85% of bladder-cancer cases up to five years before clinical diagnosis. It appeared via ScienceDaily around 21 Nov but was not cross-confirmed from a second independent primary source in this check.

USA — Common Cents Act signed into law (penny production ends)

President Trump signed the Common Cents Act (H.R. 10167) on 20 November, ending production of the one-cent coin after nearly 250 years. The law also sets rounding rules for cash transactions and requires Treasury to give Congress 60 days' notice before discontinuing any circulating coin.

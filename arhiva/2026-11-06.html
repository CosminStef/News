Fluoride-free gel that rebuilds enamel using minerals from saliva

A University of Nottingham gel is reported to pull calcium and phosphate out of saliva and guide them into new, organised crystals that rebuild tooth enamel, applied much like a fluoride treatment but without fluoride. Children — what is developing in a ~6-year-old's brain (Andrei, b. 2020)

Age 6: entering "middle childhood"

At six, a child enters what the CDC calls middle childhood (6–8). Working memory and self-control grow as the prefrontal cortex matures — the developmental reason formal schooling starts now. A typical six-year-old counts and recognises numbers, follows multi-step instructions, focuses for roughly 15 minutes, begins reading simple words, and increasingly tells imagination from reality. Socially and emotionally, friendships matter more, emotion- and conflict-management improve, independence grows, and the child puts feelings into words — while some separation anxiety and leftover fears remain normal.

Verifiedreference / evergreen
Sources: Kaiser Permanente · HealthLink BC · Columbia Doctors

What to watch for over the coming year (6 → 7)

Expect clear progress in reading and writing and in basic maths, a longer attention span, better emotional regulation (fewer meltdowns), and sustained cooperative play with 2–3 children for 20+ minutes. Flags worth raising with a pediatrician: visible regression in language or social skills, persistent school difficulty, or fears and anxiety that do not ease. Development follows a predictable sequence, but each child moves at their own pace.

Source: HealthLink BC — milestones

Education

The next 20 years vs. the classic school model

OECD — four scenarios for schooling in 2040

The OECD's "Back to the Future of Education" lays out four futures for 2040: schooling extended (today's model grows, traditional structures persist); education outsourced (learning moves outside the system); schools as learning hubs (schools reconfigured, not abolished); and learn-as-you-go (the end of school-centred learning). The OECD stresses these are deliberately fictional scenarios, not predictions.

Educating a child Andrei's age (~6) — evidence-based

Positive parenting for middle childhood

Reference guidance (CDC positive-parenting tips for 6–8, with AAP support) points to: backing growing independence while keeping predictable routines and enough sleep; "emotion coaching" — naming feelings and modelling calm so the child can co-regulate, since a six-year-old still needs an adult's help to settle, and talking things through after strong emotions pass; building healthy habits (family meals, active play) by example; and encouraging daily reading and cooperative play with other children. Clinical sources note family-centred approaches have lasting effects.

The FDA granted accelerated approval to subcutaneous amivantamab-hyaluronidase for recurrent or metastatic head-and-neck squamous cell carcinoma in patients who progressed after platinum chemotherapy and a PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor (HPV-associated oropharyngeal cancer excluded). The FDA gave "tentative" approval to Dr. Reddy's generic of twice-yearly injectable lenacapavir for HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP). Tentative approval means it can't be sold in the US; it is aimed at ~120 low- and middle-income countries at an estimated ~$40 per person per year, versus roughly $28,000 list price for Gilead's originator (Yeztugo). Dr. Reddy's is the first of six Gilead licensees to reach this milestone.

Israel's first robotic lung transplant, without opening the chest

Surgeons at Beilinson Hospital (Clalit, Petah Tikva) performed what is reported as Israel's first robotic lung transplant, using the da Vinci system to connect the donor lung through small incisions without cutting the sternum or spreading the ribs. Among roughly 20 such cases worldwide since the first in Spain three years ago, it is said to mean less post-op pain, lower infection risk and faster recovery — the patient reportedly needed little more than paracetamol.

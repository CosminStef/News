Dental

Medical / clinical discoveries

Phase III trial: silver diamine fluoride arrests early childhood cavities without drilling

A University of Michigan–led randomized trial (reported in the current news cycle, published in JAMA Pediatrics) in 830 children found that a single application of silver diamine fluoride (SDF) halted 54% of early cavities at six months, versus 22.5% with placebo, with no significant difference in reported tooth pain. SDF does not rebuild lost structure, so some teeth will still need fillings — but it offers a cheap, non-invasive first line for pediatric caries.

Lab processes & technology

Milling vs. printing for definitive restorations — hybrid labs winning

Industry analysis frames two CAD/CAM routes for final restorations: milling/grinding ceramic blocks and 3D printing light-cured resins. Printed resins still trail milled ceramics on mechanical strength (some makers add ceramic fillers to close the gap). Many labs now run hybrid models — cheaper in-house mills plus outsourcing — while additive advocates expect printing to keep encroaching on milling. Mostly vendor/trade commentary; treat performance claims as directional.

Where AI pays off first in a big practice: the back office

The clearest near-term ROI for a large clinic is administrative: AI-assisted scheduling, patient messaging/recall, documentation and insurance verification reduce staffing pressure and no-shows. Children — brain development at ~6 (Andrei, b. 2020)

What's developing now, and what to watch over the next year

Developing: At 6 the brain is pruning unused synapses and myelinating pathways, so processing speeds up; the prefrontal cortex is maturing (better impulse control, planning, multi-step instructions). Social cognition grows fast — strong interest in friendships, fairness and being accepted; vocabulary expands rapidly. Big tantrums usually ease but emotional self-regulation is still being built ("borrowing" an adult's calm first).

Watch over the next year: persistent, hard-to-settle worry; separation distress; unexplained stomachaches/headaches; sleep trouble; avoiding school or social situations — especially if it lasts weeks and disrupts sleep, school or friendships. Rule out medical causes first. Support with named feelings, calm co-regulation, steady routines, ~9–12h sleep and ≥1h active play daily. Milestones vary child to child.

Education

The next 20 years vs. the classic school

Direction of travel: personalized, outcome- and skills-based — with teachers still central

Education forecasts converge on a few shifts away from the one-pace, content-delivery classroom: AI-driven personalization becoming standard rather than novelty; a move from content-centric to outcome- and skills-based learning; and adaptive instruction that adjusts to each learner's readiness. Notably, most serious forecasters reject the "chatbot-teacher-for-everyone" vision — they frame AI as support, with human teachers, collaboration and evidence of real learning gains as the differentiators. (Caveat: credible sources mostly offer near-term 2026 outlooks; true 20-year forecasts are scarce and speculative.)

Educating a child Andrei's age (~6)

Practical approach for a 6-year-old

At this age, learning rides on play, routine and relationship. Favor hands-on, exploratory activities and real conversation over screens; keep daily routines and ~9–12h sleep steady (both underpin attention and emotional control). Coach emotions by naming them and modeling calm, allow all feelings while setting kind limits on behavior, read together daily to feed the vocabulary surge, and protect ≥1h of active outdoor play. Expect uneven progress and let curiosity, not pressure, lead.

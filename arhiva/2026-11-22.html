Psychology

Adults — new, scientifically-supported finding

Rising “intolerance of uncertainty” tracks young-adult anxiety

A meta-analysis in JAMA Psychiatry (Columbia Mailman + Boston University) pooled data on 37,000+ college-attending young adults in the US and Canada from 1992 to 2023 and found that intolerance of uncertainty — difficulty tolerating not-knowing — rose steadily, with the steepest climb after ~2012. The authors frame it as a cognitive process feeding anxiety rather than a disorder itself, and note that CBT already targets it. DSM-5 link: intolerance of uncertainty is a well-established transdiagnostic driver of anxiety disorders, most centrally Generalized Anxiety Disorder.

Children — what develops in a ~6-year-old (Andrei, b. 2020)

The maturing executive-function brain

By age six the brain is roughly 95% of adult weight, but the prefrontal cortex — the seat of planning, attention, impulse control and emotional regulation — is still immature and developing fast. Emotion regulation matures slowly, depending on strengthening links between the prefrontal cortex and the amygdala. This is also the window in which “theory of mind” (grasping that others hold different beliefs) consolidates. Evergreen developmental neuroscience, included as standing guidance.

What to watch for in the coming year (age 6→7)

Expect growing word-decoding and sight-word recognition, sustained focus for ~15 minutes, cooperative play with 2–3 peers, and firmer motor skills (catching a small ball, cutting shapes, tying laces). Raise early with the pediatrician if you see persistent reading/decoding difficulty, trouble sustaining age-appropriate attention, marked social withdrawal, or regression — early flagging improves support. Keep steady screen-time limits so they don't crowd out sleep and physical play. Pace varies by child.

The next ~20 years vs classical schooling

OECD's four scenarios to 2040

The OECD frames schooling's future not as a single forecast but as four plausible scenarios — schooling extended, education outsourced, schools as learning hubs, and learning-as-you-go. One AI-heavy scenario imagines instant assessment and certification via AI, VR/AR and IoT; others keep books, lectures and hands-on learning. The point is to build resilience against shocks, not to predict that classical schooling disappears.

Educating a child of Andrei's age (~6)

Guided play plus explicit, science-of-reading literacy

A systematic review of 39 studies supports guided play — an adult sets a learning goal and gently steers exploration — as effective for language, literacy, maths and social-emotional skills, distinct from pure free play. For reading at six, pair it with science-of-reading practice: explicit phonics and letter-sounds combined with playful, meaningful application. A blend is the best-supported approach; claims that guided play fully matches direct instruction should be read cautiously, since the evidence spans ages ~1–8.

The FDA approved Bayer's asundexian (Sunharbo), with antiplatelet therapy, to cut recurrent-stroke risk after a recent mild-to-moderate noncardioembolic ischaemic stroke — the first FXIa inhibitor cleared in the US.

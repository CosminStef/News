01 Dental

Medical discoveries

UIC wins $1.2M NIH grant to pursue dentin regrowth

A University of Illinois Chicago team led by Anne George received roughly $1.2M from the NIH to study dentin phosphophoryn, a natural tooth protein, as a way to regrow dentin. The work will use human dental-pulp stem cells and animal models to test a scaffold that could rebuild dentin inside a cavity and halt decay. This is new research funding, not yet a clinical result.

Verified3 Nov 2026 Sources: UIC · Dentistry · News-Medical

Unsweetened cranberry juice slowed plaque and acid in small trial

A small UIC randomised study (22 participants) led by Christine Wu found that unsweetened cranberry juice slowed plaque regrowth and reduced acid production compared with a sweetened cocktail or water. The effect was measured over a short 30-minute window and the study did not test actual cavity prevention. Published in Scientific Reports.

Verified28 Oct 2026 (~8 days) Sources: ScienceDaily · ScienceAlert

How English-language online dental courses sell abroad

The paid English-language CE landscape is led by implantology, followed by digital dentistry (CAD/CAM, CEREC, intraoral scanning), cosmetic/restorative, clear aligners, endodontics and practice-management topics — with succession and exit-planning webinars currently prominent. 04 Psychology

Adults — a science-backed finding, mapped to DSM-5

How insomnia, anxiety and depression interconnect in early pregnancy

A network-analysis study, described by its authors as the first to model how anxiety, depression and insomnia symptoms link together in early pregnancy, screened pregnant women and found roughly 17% positive for insomnia, 8% for depression and 6% for anxiety. Tension, worry and low mood emerged as central symptoms bridging the three clusters, suggesting useful targets for early screening. Maps to DSM-5: Insomnia Disorder, Generalized Anxiety Disorder, Major Depressive Disorder. Published in Frontiers in Psychiatry; full text was not opened to confirm all details.

Partial3 Nov 2026 Source: Frontiers in Psychiatry (DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1957627)

Fear learning blurs "danger" and "safety" maps in the hippocampus

In mice, fear learning made the brain's representations of a dangerous and a safe environment become more similar in the ventral hippocampus, while the dorsal hippocampus kept them distinct. The authors interpret this overlap as a speed-for-precision trade-off that enables faster threat recall and propose it as a candidate mechanism for the overgeneralised fear seen in PTSD and anxiety disorders. This is an animal study, not yet shown in humans; the journal paper is from September but drew fresh press this week.

Verifiedjournal 14 Sep 2026; press ~2 Nov 2026 Sources: Nature Neuroscience · McGill

Children — the ~6-year-old brain (Andrei, born 2020)

Reference / evergreen, grounded in established developmental science (APA, physician-reviewed sources) — what is developing now and what to watch for over the coming year.

A leap in reasoning and memory (ages ~6–8)

Around this age a child internalises rules, can hold several parts of a problem in mind, sustains attention for longer and begins planning simple steps before acting — making it a prime time to teach problem-solving, as "magical thinking" fades. Watch for persistent trouble following two-step instructions or attention that is very short relative to peers.

Verifiedreference Source: APA

Executive function is maturing — but far from finished

Ages 6–7 are a key window for attention and self-control: children get better at delaying gratification, holding conditional rules in mind and thinking flexibly, while the prefrontal cortex keeps developing into the mid-20s. Watch for trouble starting or finishing tasks, working-memory lapses, or emotional dysregulation that can look like "laziness" or defiance.

Verifiedreference Sources: Sesame Workshop · Understood

Emerging reading, writing and early-math skills

Typical six-year-olds begin reading, do simple arithmetic and write short sentences. This is the year to watch for early signs of a reading or learning difficulty — such as persistent struggle linking letters to sounds — and to raise any concern at the well-child visit, along with routine vision and hearing checks and at least 60 minutes of daily activity plus protected sleep.

Verifiedreference Source: KidsHealth (6-year checkup)

05 Education

Education over the next ~20 years vs. classical schooling

OECD's four scenarios for schooling to 2040

The OECD's "Back to the Future of Education" sets out four alternative futures: schooling expands much as today; it is partly outsourced to education markets; schools are repurposed as community learning hubs; or school-based learning dissolves into ubiquitous, technology-mediated learning. It is explicitly a planning tool rather than a prediction.

Verifiedframework to 2040 Source: OECD

WEF: 39% of core skills to shift by 2030

The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 projects around 170 million new roles and 92 million displaced by 2030, a net gain, and estimates that 39% of workers' core skills will change or become outdated between 2025 and 2030 — with AI, big data, cybersecurity and digital literacy growing fastest. The implication for education is a shift toward continuous reskilling and AI literacy rather than one-time credentials.

Verified2025 (horizon 2030) Source: World Economic Forum

Consensus trend: skills over degrees, AI-personalised and hybrid

Across 2025–2026 education commentary, recurring forecasts include competency-based learning and micro-credentials rising relative to seat-time degrees, blended/hybrid models becoming standard, AI moving from administration into adaptive personalised instruction, and growing emphasis on social-emotional learning. Specific percentage claims in these pieces are largely unsourced, so treat the direction — not the numbers — as the signal.

Unverified (trade commentary)2025–2026 Source: Faculty Focus

Educating a ~6-year-old (Andrei, born 2020)

Reference / evidence-based guidance (AAP, APA, peer-reviewed).

Read aloud daily — and keep it interactive

The AAP treats shared, engaging read-aloud as a core practice that builds language, a love of reading and school readiness, and it stays valuable even as a child starts reading independently. Pairing early literacy with early-math exposure strengthens both.

Verifiedreference Source: AAP

Coach problem-solving and emotions explicitly

Because 6–8 is when children can internalise rules and reason through steps, it is a good age to model and practise problem-solving. "Emotion coaching" parenting approaches have controlled-trial evidence for improving how parents coach children aged 5–6, though the trial base is modest.

Verified / Partialreference Sources: APA · peer-reviewed trial

Protect sleep, movement and structured screen time

For ages six and up, AAP and AACAP advise structuring screen media so it does not displace sleep, exercise, play, reading and family time, and ensuring at least 60 minutes of daily physical activity — all linked to better attention and self-regulation. Favour play and hands-on activity over passive screens as a learning channel.

Verifiedreference Sources: AACAP · KidsHealth

06 Medicine

New discoveries & firsts (science-backed)

First FDA-approved plasmid-DNA immunotherapy (Voceryq)

The FDA granted accelerated approval to Inovio's Voceryq for adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis, a chronic HPV-driven airway disease, delivered via the Cellectra device. The agency describes it as the first plasmid DNA-based immunotherapy approved in the US. Approval rested on a small study in which about 27% of patients needed no surgery over 12 months, and is conditional on a confirmatory trial.

Verified30 Oct 2026 (~6 days) Sources: Pharmacy Times · Contagion Live

Phase 3 results for a potential first drug for pterygium

Cloudbreak Pharma reported that both doses of its eye-drop candidate CBT-001 met the primary endpoints in a 659-patient Phase 3 trial, significantly reducing pterygium lesion-length progression at 12 months and eye redness at 3 months versus vehicle. If eventually approved it could become the first drug (non-surgical) treatment for pterygium, a growth on the eye surface. These are unaudited company topline figures, not yet peer-reviewed or FDA-reviewed.

Partial5 Nov 2026 Sources: GlobeNewswire · TipRanks

Lorundrostat lowers blood pressure in sleep-apnoea patients

At the AHA Hypertension Scientific Sessions, Mineralys presented data on its aldosterone-synthase inhibitor lorundrostat showing a roughly 11 mmHg drop in morning systolic blood pressure in patients with obstructive sleep apnoea and hypertension, with no significant potassium safety signal. Notably, the study missed its sleep-apnoea endpoint, so the finding supports a blood-pressure role rather than treating the sleep disorder; an FDA decision for hypertension is expected in late December.

Partial4 Nov 2026 Sources: Mineralys IR · MyChesCo

Annamycin interim leukaemia data accepted for ASH 2026

Moleculin said interim data from its MIRACLE trial of Annamycin plus cytarabine in relapsed/refractory acute myeloid leukaemia was accepted for presentation at the American Society of Hematology meeting, reporting complete-remission rates of 43% and 36% in the two Annamycin arms versus 12% for placebo among the first 45 randomised patients. This is a small, preliminary interim analysis from a single company source.

Medical / clinical discoveries

Silver diamine fluoride stops over half of early-childhood cavities in Phase III trial

A Phase III trial led by University of Michigan researchers, reported in JAMA Pediatrics, tested the topical liquid silver diamine fluoride (SDF) on 830 children aged 12–71 months with severe early-childhood decay. At six months SDF had arrested 54.0% of cavities versus 22.5% on placebo. It halts decay but does not rebuild lost tooth structure, so some lesions still need fillings.

Children — brain development around age 6 (for Andrei, b. 2020)

What's developing at ~6: cause-and-effect, time, and fading "magical thinking"

Around age six the preschool-era "magical thinking" fades quickly and children start to grasp cause-and-effect and the concept of time. Typical markers include reading some simple words, telling their age, and catching a ball — but pace varies widely, so checklists are guides, not tests. The strongest lever on brain development at this age remains responsive, back-and-forth interaction with caregivers (talking, listening, naming emotions), plus daily physical play and limited screen time.

Verifieddevelopmental guidance Columbia Doctors Kaiser Permanente

What to watch in the coming year

Expect growth in cause-and-effect reasoning, early reading and number sense, and emotional self-regulation. Flag with a pediatrician any persistent trouble following two-step instructions, forming peer friendships, or regulating emotions well beyond same-age peers. For authoritative milestone red-flags, the CDC's "Learn the Signs. Act Early." and the AAP are the right references.

Psychology

—Adults — a science-backed finding, read against DSM-5

Depression may not be one disorder: five distinct brain-activity profiles

Using magnetoencephalography, a University of Helsinki team compared 263 adults with depression against 75 controls and found five subgroups with different patterns of connectivity between brain regions — some stronger than normal, some weaker. Because the single DSM-5 label of Major Depressive Disorder maps onto nearly opposite brain signatures, the authors argue depression is biologically heterogeneous, which could explain conflicting imaging studies and uneven treatment response. It directly challenges MDD as a unitary category and points toward biologically-defined subtypes — but it is still investigational, not a clinical test.

How cortisol strengthens emotional memories

A Yale study showed that the stress hormone cortisol reshapes communication between the brain's emotion and memory networks, making memory formation more selective for emotionally intense events. —Children — the brain of a ~6-year-old (Andrei, b. 2020)

What is developing right now

Age six marks the move into middle childhood: cause-and-effect reasoning sharpens, preschool "magical thinking" fades, and sustained attention lengthens to roughly 15 minutes at a stretch — the brain becoming ready for formal instruction, simple reading and basic arithmetic. Fine-motor control matures too (tying shoes, copying shapes, using scissors), and most children start losing baby teeth.

evergreen developmental scienceVerified
kaiserpermanente.org columbiadoctors.org

What to watch for in the coming year (6 → 7)

Per CDC guidance for ages 6–8, expect growing independence, more future-oriented thinking, and a sharp rise in the importance of friendships, teamwork and peer acceptance. Social cognition turns outward — less self-focus, more empathy, and a dawning grasp of fairness, rules and self-conscious emotions like guilt and embarrassment. Watch for peer sensitivity (wanting to be liked) and competitiveness, and raise any lagging milestone at the paediatric check-up, since early intervention matters.

Education

—The next ~20 years vs. the classical school

The OECD's four scenarios for schooling in 2040

The OECD's "Back to the Future of Education" sets out four plausible futures — explicitly thought-experiments, not predictions: schooling extended (formal schools persist and grow digitally); education outsourced (learning fragments across private and community providers); schools as learning hubs (schools open up as community anchors); and learn-as-you-go (rigid structures dissolve into continuous, AI-enabled, on-demand learning). Its parallel 2030 Learning Compass stresses the shift from content coverage toward transferable competencies and student agency.

—Educating a child of Andrei's age (~6)

Evidence-based approaches for ages 6–8

CDC positive-parenting guidance recommends talking with the child about school, friendships and plans; setting clear, consistent expectations; encouraging independence (self-dressing, chores, goal-setting); supporting friendships and teamwork; and keeping reading and conversation central, since language underpins emotional self-regulation. A 2026 systematic review of ages 4–6 adds that structured or "mindful" play, role-play, storytelling and emotion-coaching reliably improve regulation, impulse control, cognitive flexibility and empathy — with the strongest effects when a supportive adult scaffolds the activity (co-regulation).

Medicine

Chemo-free regimen beats chemotherapy in Philadelphia-positive leukaemia (NEJM)

The phase-3 GIMEMA ALL2820 trial randomised 236 adults with newly diagnosed Ph-positive acute lymphoblastic leukaemia to a chemotherapy-free combination of ponatinib plus blinatumomab versus imatinib plus chemotherapy. The targeted-plus-immunotherapy arm was superior on event-free survival (roughly 86% vs 67% at 24 months) with higher complete hematologic response. The authors caution that overall-survival differences were smaller and follow-up is limited; this is the full publication of data first shown at ASH in December 2025.

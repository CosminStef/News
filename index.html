Psychology

Adults — one scientifically proven item (DSM-5)

EEG "phase-resetting" dynamics track depression severity

A University of Tsukuba team recorded EEG from 276 untreated adults and measured how phase-resetting in one brain region shifts the chance of resetting in another. Combined across region pairs, these cross-region dynamics tracked depressive-symptom severity and distinguished moderate-or-higher from milder symptoms at roughly 80% accuracy — a possible objective biomarker for major depressive disorder (DSM-5), pending clinical validation.

Sources: Nature Mental Health · University of Tsukuba
Children — Andrei (born 2020, ~6 years old)

The 6-year-old brain: prefrontal refinement and the leap to logical thinking

Around age 6 the brain is in rapid synaptic refinement and prefrontal-cortex growth, driving gains in executive function (working memory, impulse control, focus) and a shift from preschool "magical thinking" toward logical cause-and-effect reasoning. Expect emerging literacy and numeracy, a firmer grasp of real vs. imaginary, growing empathy and the ability to recognise two emotions at once — but with still-immature emotional regulation, so frustration and mood swings remain normal.

Watch over the coming year (6→7): consolidation of reading fluency, lengthening attention span, more cooperative friendships, better self-regulation. Flag with a pediatrician if you see persistent trouble with reading/letters, inability to follow multi-step instructions, marked social withdrawal, or regulation far behind peers.

How to educate a ~6-year-old (Andrei)

Play-based learning plus structured emotion coaching

At 6, play-based learning is among the most effective approaches — it gives natural practice in emotional regulation, cooperation and empathy, and works best paired with debriefing ("what made you happy/frustrated in that game?"). Build literacy and numeracy through short, concrete activities alongside daily shared reading; adults should model calm regulation and create safe space for feelings. A randomized "I Spy Feelings" intervention for 5–6-year-olds shows structured emotion-regulation programs produce measurable short-term gains at this age.

Medicine

New discoveries & firsts (scientifically proven)

Glioblastoma follows predictable "rules" — two companion Nature papers

Two Nature studies show glioblastoma's cell-state switching — long assumed essentially random — actually follows a predictable, spatially patterned program. One introduces scDORI, a deep-learning tool that mapped gene-regulatory networks across more than a million cells; reprogramming a single regulator improved survival in mouse models. The second built a multi-omic atlas of 12 tumors showing heterogeneity traces to a stereotyped developmental-like trajectory, opening potential new therapeutic targets.

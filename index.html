4Psychology

Adults — new, science-backed (relative to DSM-5)

Symptom clusters may beat DSM-5 labels for treating anxiety & depression

A network analysis of 33,675 patients on a message-based therapy platform identified four cross-cutting symptom clusters — affective dysregulation, worries, neurovegetative symptoms and hyperarousal — rather than the clean anxiety-vs-depression split of DSM-5 categories. In a 10,718-patient subsample tracked over 12 weeks, the affective-dysregulation and neurovegetative clusters predicted poorer outcomes while the worries and hyperarousal clusters predicted recovery. The takeaway: matching treatment to a person's dominant symptom pattern may work better than treating the diagnostic label alone.

Verified26 Oct 2026· Northeastern University · MedicalXpress

Theta-burst stimulation helps late-life depression — but needs time

A sham-controlled randomised trial (108 adults, average age 67) of bilateral theta-burst stimulation over the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex found the active treatment significantly beat sham by week 12, but not yet by week 6 — older patients with (often treatment-resistant) major depression respond, but need a longer course. Relevant to DSM-5 Major Depressive Disorder.

VerifiedOct 2026· AAPP · Psychiatric News

Children — Andrei (born 2020, ~6): the brain this year & what to watch

Executive function and "theory of mind" take a leap at 5–6

Between roughly 3 and 5 a child's executive-function skills (working memory, impulse control, cognitive flexibility) develop fastest, and research shows these gains drive the growth of "theory of mind" — understanding that other people hold their own thoughts, beliefs and feelings. By 5–6 a child can separate their own mental model from someone else's and grasp that beliefs can be false; understanding emotions caused by false beliefs usually consolidates around 6–7. Thinking shifts from fantasy toward logic, and most children can follow multi-step directions, focus ~15 minutes, take turns, count, and begin reading simple words.

Verified· CDC milestones (5 yrs) · PMC: executive function

What to watch for over the coming year

Milestone timing varies widely, so keep the range in mind before worrying. Worth a calm, non-diagnostic check with the paediatrician or teacher: by age 6, no recognition of letters or numbers, little interest in print or persistent trouble recognising letters, learning that is markedly harder than same-age peers, or language that sounds noticeably younger than his age. The CDC stresses its checklist is not a substitute for a validated developmental screen — ask for one if concerned.

Verified· CDC

How to educate a child of Andrei's age (born 2020)

Play-based learning is the evidence-backed route

For 5–6-year-olds, intentional, adult-supported play is one of the strongest ways to build executive function — which in turn predicts later academic success (one longitudinal study found working memory at age 5 predicted academic performance six years later better than IQ). Research-tested tools include games that train impulse control and flexible thinking — Simon Says, red-light/green-light, card-sorting, musical freeze, building blocks — plus rich pretend play. The adult's active role (following the child's lead, naming feelings, light structure) matters more than drilling academics.

Verified· Play-based EF program (PMC) · Frontiers in Psychology

Daily reading routines and sensible screen limits

Keep daily reading routines — read to him and have him read to you — to support the emerging reading and language skills of this age. On media, AAP guidance for ages 6+ is to set consistent limits on amount and type so screens don't displace sleep, activity and family time: no devices in the bedroom at night, media off during meals and homework. (Specific "1–2 hour" caps circulate but are not AAP's own wording; current guidance traces to the 2016 policy and Family Media Use Plan.)

FDA expands talazoparib + enzalutamide to earlier-stage prostate cancer

The FDA approved talazoparib (Talzenna) with enzalutamide (Xtandi) for adults with metastatic, hormone-sensitive prostate cancer carrying HRR gene mutations — extending the 2023 approval that was limited to castration-resistant disease. In the Phase 3 TALAPRO-3 trial (599 patients), median radiographic progression-free survival was not reached with the combination vs 45.8 months for enzalutamide plus placebo (HR 0.48; P<.0001); overall-survival data were still immature.

Dental medical discoveries & research

Protein gel rebuilds tooth enamel using minerals from saliva

A fluoride-free protein gel from University of Nottingham researchers seeps into enamel microcracks and pulls minerals out of saliva, organizing them into new aligned crystals. On 32 extracted molars it rebuilt layers up to ~10 microns in two weeks. Results are lab-only, not clinical. Note: the underlying Nature Communications study dates to 2025; Nov 2026 coverage is largely a re-packaging.

PartialStudy 2025; recirculated ~Nov 4–5, 2026

4 · Psychology

Adults

Loneliness and isolation track with faster biological aging

A large UK study (>300,000 adults) found that socially isolated and lonely people show an "older" biological age than their birthdate predicts, with a somewhat higher risk of death. Effects per unit were small, but people with both high genetic risk and high isolation were biologically ~1.7 years older. The design is observational, so it shows association, not causation. DSM-5 link: loneliness is an established risk/maintenance factor for Major Depressive Disorder and anxiety disorders.

Verified2026 (mortality strand covered ~Sept 25, 2026)
Sources: PsyPost · UK Biobank publication

One week of less social media eases depression and anxiety in distressed young adults

A randomized trial had young adults (18–24) cut social-media use for one week; anxiety fell ~16%, depression ~24% and insomnia ~14.5%, with the largest gains among those already more depressed. Loneliness did not change, and the authors stress it is not a standalone treatment. Note: the primary JAMA Network Open study is late 2025; it was re-reported on Nov 18, 2026 (within the window).

VerifiedStudy ~Nov 2025; re-report Nov 18, 2026
Sources: U.S. News / HealthDay · NPR

Children — Andrei (~6, born 2020): brain development & what to watch

Cognitive and social milestones around age 6 (first-grade transition)

Around age 6, children move from preschool "magical thinking" toward concrete cause-and-effect reasoning; they grasp numbers to ~10, begin to understand time, and rapidly develop reading and writing (with a wide normal range). Socially they sustain back-and-forth conversation with peers and gain self-care independence (tying shoes, using a knife). Note: neither CDC nor AAP publishes a formal age-6 screening checklist — age-6 guidance comes from AAP Bright Futures and clinical consensus.

What to watch this year (6→7): consolidation of phonics into fluent reading; emerging number sense and simple time concepts; longer attention span and rule-based play; friendships and emotional regulation. Seek a developmental check if he consistently struggles to recognize letters/sounds, follow instructions, be understood, or make friends.

Verified / evergreenAAP Bright Futures; accessed Nov 2026
Sources: AAP Bright Futures (PDF) · HealthyChildren.org (AAP)

5 · Education

Forecasts: the next ~20 years vs. classical schooling

OECD "Back to the Future of Education" — four 2040 scenarios

The OECD's foresight framework lays out four deliberately fictional scenarios for schooling by 2040: (1) schooling extended, (2) education outsourced, (3) schools as learning hubs, (4) learn-as-you-go. It makes no prediction or recommendation — the point is structured discussion. It notes "the place called school" remains dominant despite decades of change, and that grading may not capture gains from personalized, adaptive learning.

VerifiedOECD framework (2020); accessed Nov 2026
Sources: OECD (full report)

AI-driven personalization — directional consensus, weak evidence base

The recurring forecast is a shift from one-teacher-to-30 toward AI-supported individualized learning paths, with teachers becoming "learning experience designers," continuous lifelong learning, and changed assessment. Human interaction is still seen as essential (blended models favored). Caveats matter: most sources are opinion/vendor pieces; an arXiv analysis flags algorithmic-bias risk, and strong AI-tutor claims could not be verified against primary papers.

Partial2024–2026 range
Sources: arXiv analysis · arXiv (rural-India LLM study)

How to educate a child of Andrei's age (~6)

Evidence-based support for a 6-year-old's learning

AAP guidance: kindergarten screening should guide instruction, not gatekeep, and screen-based apps/shows must not replace face-to-face interaction and real-life experience. Practical, play-based supports: make reading a daily habit and have him write or talk about stories; weave everyday number practice into daily life; move from decodable texts toward easy chapter books with targeted phonics; use pretend play, movement, drawing/building, board games, conversation and small chores. Expect wide variation and that tasks often need to be slowed or repeated.

6 · Medicine

New discoveries & firsts

AML cancer vaccine (galinpepimut-S) meets Phase 3 primary endpoint

SELLAS Life Sciences announced that its pivotal Phase 3 REGAL trial of the WT1-targeting vaccine galinpepimut-S hit its primary overall-survival endpoint in acute myeloid leukemia. Among transplant-ineligible patients in 2nd+ remission (n=126), median OS was 24.0 vs 14.7 months (HR 0.59, p=0.035). The company plans FDA filing talks and a BLA in H1 2027. Caveats: topline company data, open-label design, p-value near threshold.

VerifiedNov 17, 2026
Sources: Washington Post · MSKCC

Gene therapy into the hippocampus cuts drug-resistant seizures >90%

A first-in-human, NEJM-published trial delivered a gene therapy directly into a small hippocampal region in three adults with severe treatment-resistant epilepsy; monthly seizures dropped more than 90%. The sample is very small and all three stayed on existing antiseizure medication, so the effect cannot be fully isolated; larger trials are planned.

VerifiedNEJM ~Nov 6; re-covered Nov 19, 2026
Sources: Singularity Hub · bdnews24

Blood biomarker for CTE in living people (eMTBR-tau243)

A WashU-led team reported that eMTBR-tau243, an Alzheimer's-linked tau fragment, is elevated in people with advanced CTE — a step toward diagnosing in life a disease currently confirmable only at autopsy. Limits are important: it did not reliably detect early-stage CTE, blood came from only 11 classified individuals, and a diagnostic test in life is not yet available. Some coverage conflates this with separate Boston University work.

PartialNov 16–19, 2026
Sources: NBC Boston · Science News

Spryte Medical reported that the first FDA-approved pivotal trial (INSYTE) of an intravascular optical imaging device used inside the brain's blood vessels met its primary effectiveness endpoint with favorable safety, during treatment of intracranial aneurysms. Announced at SVIN 2026. Source is a company release carried on wires — not yet independent peer review.

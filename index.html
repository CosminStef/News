Psychology

Adults — a proven finding, related to DSM-5

Fear blurs the brain's "danger" vs "safety" maps

A McGill University team reported in Nature Neuroscience that, in mice, fear causes the brain's danger and safety memory maps to overlap instead of staying distinct — an over-active survival mechanism that may underlie the over-generalised fear seen in anxiety disorders and PTSD. It reframes PTSD-type fear as a failure to separate safe from dangerous cues rather than simply "too much" fear. Note this is mechanistic animal research, not yet a human clinical finding.

Verified ~Oct 15, 2026 · recirculated Nov 2 ScienceDaily · McGill release

GLP-1 drugs linked to fewer alcohol-related hospitalizations

A Swedish national-register, within-individual study in The Lancet Psychiatry found that periods when the same patients were on GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., semaglutide) had fewer alcohol- and substance-related hospitalizations than their untreated periods; a separate BMJ Open trial-emulation study of 40,703 adults with alcohol use disorder found 26–65% fewer alcohol-related admissions. Both are observational, so causation is not established and randomized trials are still needed.

Partial (observational) 2026 News-Medical · Lancet Psychiatry / BMJ Open

Children — Andrei (born 2020, turning 6)

The "5-to-7 shift": a leap in self-control around age six

Between roughly five and seven, children make marked gains in executive function — controlling attention, impulses and behaviour — a well-documented transition tied both to brain maturation and to school entry. A typical six-year-old can follow multi-step instructions, sustain attention for about 15 minutes, use largely correct grammar, and grasp basic rules and fairness. Theory of mind (understanding others' thoughts and feelings) also strengthens and appears to support emotional regulation.

Verified (behavioral) Evidence base: Brod, Bunge & Shing, Psychological Science (2017) Psychological Science · health-system milestone guides
What to watch this coming year: big feelings are normal — flag a persistent inability to calm or recover; some separation anxiety and classic fears (dark, animals) are still age-typical. Expect ~15-minute focused tasks and three-part instructions, cooperative play with 2–3 children, mostly correct grammar, and writing his name and some letters/numbers. Uneven development across domains is normal; raise persistent concerns with a pediatrician rather than a checklist.

Education

The next 20 years vs. the classical school

Four OECD scenarios for schooling in 2040

The OECD's "Back to the Future of Education" sets out four alternative futures for 2040 that challenge the factory-model school: schooling extended, education outsourced, schools as learning hubs, and learn-as-you-go — with digital tech and AI enabling personalised, analytics-driven learning. The OECD stresses these are deliberately non-predictive thinking tools, not forecasts. Converging commentary points to personalisation, hybrid delivery and lifelong learning displacing standardised age-cohort instruction.

Verified (OECD primary) OECD report (framework in continued use 2026) OECD
A widely cited WEF Future of Jobs figure holds that ~40% of workers' core skills will change or become outdated by 2030, used to argue that a standardised curriculum is mismatched to future work. Partial — cited second-hand; confirm against the primary WEF report before quoting.

Educating a six-year-old — evidence-based

Play, read, and guard sleep

Current American Academy of Pediatrics guidance for ages 6–12 drops rigid screen-time numbers in favour of "crowding in" sleep, physical activity, play, reading and family time, and choosing quality content (turn off autoplay and infinite scroll). Because six is the executive-function and theory-of-mind window, experts favour play-based, hands-on learning, daily shared reading, strategy and puzzle games, and consistent routines over heavy screens or rote drilling. Naming feelings and modelling calm supports stronger self-control and peer acceptance.

Medicine

Context: the dominant event in this window was the American Heart Association Scientific Sessions 2026 (Chicago, Nov 6–9), with several results published simultaneously in the New England Journal of Medicine on Nov 7.

Pelacarsen cuts Lp(a) by 78% but not cardiovascular events (Lp(a)HORIZON)

Full phase-3 results for Novartis/Ionis's pelacarsen, presented at AHA 2026 and published in NEJM, confirmed the drug lowered lipoprotein(a) by about 78% but did not reduce major cardiovascular events in 8,323 patients with elevated Lp(a) and established cardiovascular disease. All-cause death was 5.7% vs 5.9%, with no benefit in any subgroup — a landmark negative result that challenges the Lp(a) hypothesis for this agent. Investigators say it is not the end of the Lp(a) story.

Verified Nov 7, 2026 TCTMD · NEJM · HCPLive

Intensive blood-pressure control after ischemic stroke reduces events (OPTIMAL-Stroke)

A randomized trial of 4,368 patients with a prior ischemic stroke or TIA across 30 Brazilian sites found that targeting systolic blood pressure below 120 mm Hg cut major cardiovascular events versus a below-140 target (10.9% vs 13.5%). Serious adverse events were similar between arms, though kidney-related outcomes and hyponatremia were modestly more frequent with intensive treatment. Presented at AHA 2026 and published in NEJM on Nov 7.

Verified Nov 7, 2026 HCPLive · NEJM · ACC

Oral PCSK9 inhibitor enlicitide: durable LDL lowering across risk groups

New analyses of Merck's enlicitide — the first FDA-approved oral PCSK9 inhibitor (approved July 2026) — were presented at AHA 2026. A CORALreef Lipids analysis showed LDL-C reductions of ~57–63% held consistently regardless of baseline cardiovascular risk, and an open-label extension showed the effect maintained through ~104 weeks. Cardiovascular-outcomes data are not expected until around 2029.

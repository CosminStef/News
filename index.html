Courses & the online English-language dentistry course market

No dated news event in the last 24h. Market assessment below (context, not breaking news).

How online English-language dental CE is sold internationally

English-language continuing education is sold mostly direct to dentists through three channels: subscription/membership platforms (e.g., Viva Learning, Dental Learning — ADA CERP / AGD PACE accredited); manufacturer "academies" that use free online courses as a funnel into paid hands-on training (e.g., Dentsply Sirona Aligner Course Series); and individual thought-leader brands selling high-ticket self-paced plus hands-on bundles (commonly ~$3,000–$4,000, e.g., 3D Dentists, Implant Pathways). The best-selling topics are implants/surgery, clear aligners/orthodontics, and practice marketing; accreditation is the key trust signal buyers look for.

Roughly two in three tweens and teens now use AI chatbots

Two surveys released around Oct 30 — a JED Foundation survey of 5,500 students aged 9–17 (90% had used an AI chatbot) and a Common Sense Media survey — found heavy chatbot use among minors, with certain uses correlating with worse mental-health symptoms. The data are cross-sectional, so causation is unclear; an independent developmental psychologist cautioned against over-reading the link.

VerifiedOct 30, 2026

Depression may be five distinct brain "profiles," not one disorder

A University of Helsinki team used MEG recordings from 263 adults with major depressive disorder plus 75 controls and sorted patients into five subtypes based on brain-connectivity patterns, which differed in symptoms and in hyper- versus hypo-connectivity. The work supports the view that DSM-5's single MDD category may mask biologically distinct conditions, though it is cross-sectional and needs replication.

Verified~Sept 23, 2026 (outside window)
Sources: University of Helsinki · Neuroscience News

Deeper slow-wave sleep linked to more anxiety in young adults

A study in the journal Sleep found that healthy young male conscripts with a higher proportion of deep (N3) sleep and slower dissipation of slow-wave activity reported greater anxiety — counter to the usual assumption that more deep sleep is always better. The sample was young men only, so it may not generalize.

Partial~Oct 14, 2026
Single reporting outlet located; DOI recommended for confirmation.
Source: DocWire News

Children — brain development of a ~6-year-old (Andrei, b. 2020)

Prefrontal cortex and myelination drive attention and self-control

Around age 6 the frontal cortex (attention, problem-solving, impulse control) is still maturing and myelination continues, speeding communication between brain regions. The practical result: a 6-year-old can focus longer (roughly 15 minutes on a task), follow multi-step instructions and filter distractions better than a preschooler. Watch for in the coming year: impulse control is growing but still limited, so meltdowns and difficulty waiting remain developmentally normal.

VerifiedEstablished science
Sources: Palomar — Child Development · Kaiser Permanente

Concrete reasoning and early literacy/numeracy take off

By 6, children typically count to and understand "10," recognize numbers, sort and compare objects, begin sounding out and reading simple words, and increasingly distinguish imagination from reality. Expect rapid gains in reading fluency and basic arithmetic this year as formal (first-grade) schooling begins.

VerifiedEstablished science
Sources: Columbia Doctors · Scholastic

Friendships, fairness and lingering childhood fears

Six-year-olds grow in cooperative play, want to follow rules and please adults, and become sensitive to fairness; many still have age-typical fears (the dark, monsters, large animals). Watch this year for emotional regulation under school pressure and whether friendships and group play are developing — persistent social withdrawal is worth discussing with a pediatrician.

VerifiedEstablished science
Sources: PeaceHealth · WebMD

Education over the next ~20 years vs. classic schooling

OECD's four scenarios for schooling to 2040

The OECD's "Back to the Future of Education" lays out four futures to 2040 — Schooling Extended, Education Outsourced, Schools as Learning Hubs, and Learn-as-you-go — ranging from today's school model persisting to learning dispersed across digital platforms and communities. It is explicitly a planning framework, not a prediction, showing classic schooling is one of several plausible paths rather than a given.

VerifiedOECD report
Source: OECD (PDF)

Generative AI will reshape classrooms (OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026)

The OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 reviews evidence on generative AI in student self-study, joint student–teacher use and teacher-only use, concluding that AI can aid learning when guided by sound teaching principles and that outright bans are not sustainable. The direction of travel is classic instruction increasingly augmented by AI tutoring and automation rather than replaced.

VerifiedJan 2026
Source: OECD

The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 states employers expect 39% of workers' core skills to change by 2030 (down from 44% in 2023), implying schooling and lifelong learning must shift toward adaptability, AI literacy and continuous reskilling rather than fixed curricula.

How to educate a child of Andrei's age (~6)

Daily shared reading and word games

At 6, children sound out simple words using letter sounds and context; daily shared reading expands vocabulary and word games build decoding skills, and a parent's own visible enjoyment of reading has a lasting effect. It is low-cost and directly matched to this age's emerging literacy.

Verified
Source: PBS Parents

Match tasks to attention span; use multi-step instructions

Because a 6-year-old can typically focus about 15 minutes and follow multi-step directions, keep learning sessions short and concrete, build in movement, and gradually increase complexity as frontal-lobe control matures. Pair this with clear, consistent routines to support still-developing impulse control.

Verified
Source: Scholastic

Limit screens; prioritize interaction and sleep

Pediatric guidance for this age recommends reading together and limiting screen time, with real-world interaction and adequate sleep supporting attention and emotional regulation. Given the recent findings on children and AI chatbots, be especially deliberate about AI and companion-app exposure for a child this young.

Medicine

FDA approves Voceryq — first plasmid-DNA immunotherapy for recurrent respiratory papillomatosis

The FDA granted accelerated approval to Inovio's Voceryq (INO-3107) for adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis, a rare HPV-driven disease that causes wart-like airway growths. Described as the first plasmid-DNA-based immunotherapy of its kind, the approval rested on a reduction in the number of surgeries needed, with confirmatory trials required. It is Inovio's first approved product.

VerifiedOct 30, 2026
Sources: FDA · Reuters-sourced wire

Diet plus supervised exercise put early-onset type 2 diabetes into remission in 54%

In the randomized RESET for Remission trial, adults under 45 with early-onset type 2 diabetes followed an 800–900 kcal/day low-energy diet plus structured exercise. At 24 weeks, 54% achieved remission versus 4% on usual care. A key limitation is the absence of a diet-only arm, so exercise's independent contribution is unclear, and long-term durability is unknown.

VerifiedOct 29–30, 2026
Sources: The Lancet Regional Health · ScienceDaily

Oxaloacetate may ease cancer-related "brain fog"

A small UCLA Health-led study tested oxaloacetate, a molecule used in the body's energy metabolism, in breast-cancer survivors with cancer-related cognitive impairment. After eight weeks, participants reported improvements in memory and concentration. The authors cautioned the sample was small with no placebo group; a randomized placebo-controlled phase 2 trial is planned.

Verified~Oct 29–30, 2026
Sources: Technology Networks · BCRF

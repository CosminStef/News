1 Dental

Medical / scientific discoveries

NIH funds research into a tooth protein that could regrow dentine

A University of Illinois Chicago team won a ~$1.2M grant from the NIH's dental-research institute to study dentin phosphophoryn (DPP), a protein that may let damaged teeth rebuild their own dentine. The plan is a protein scaffold placed in a cavity to form a living matrix instead of a conventional filling, exploring DPP's role in cellular "recycling" (autophagy) using human pulp stem cells and animal models.

Verified ~Nov 2, 2026 · News-Medical, Oral Health Group

Review reframes early cavities as reversible, not inevitably "drill-and-fill"

A peer-reviewed narrative review in Dentistry Journal argues that early caries lesions are biologically active and can be arrested or reversed by restoring the mineral/biofilm balance at the tooth surface. It is a synthesis of existing evidence rather than new experimental data, but it reinforces a prevention-first clinical posture.

Context: DSM-5 categories may not match the mind's natural symptom clusters

A large data-driven reanalysis of self-reported symptoms from ~14,700+ people statistically reorganized DSM-5 symptoms and found several classic categories — major depression, generalized anxiety, PTSD — do not emerge as clean, distinct syndromes, supporting a more dimensional, symptom-level model. A 2026 University of Kansas veterans analysis reached a similar conclusion. Note: this is well-verified but older than the 24–48h window, offered as background.

Verified study 2024–25 · corroboration May 2026 · Univ. of Kansas

Children — a 6-year-old's brain (Andrei, b. 2020)

What is developing around age 6, and what to watch this year

Developing now: rapid gains in working memory, attention and problem-solving (prefrontal maturation driving self-regulation and impulse control); early literacy and numeracy (reading simple words, counting past 20, beginning addition/subtraction); deeper friendships, perspective-taking and a strong sense of fairness/rules; and refined fine-motor control for writing and drawing.

Flags to raise with a pediatrician: can't follow simple two-step instructions or shows little interest in letters/numbers/stories; persistent trouble making friends or regulating emotions well beyond peers; consistent teacher concerns about learning. Media note: 2026 pediatric guidance links misused screen media in ages 6–12 to poorer sleep and weaker attention — watch that screens don't crowd out sleep, activity, homework and family time.

Verified developmental consensus · AAP, Columbia Doctors

5 Education

Education over the next ~20 years vs. classic schooling

Forecasts converge on personalized, competency-based, AI-augmented learning

Several 2026 institutional forecasts point the same way. The Open University's Future of Learning report argues formal education must flex to fast technological change and shifting skills; KnowledgeWorks maps a decade of structural K-12 change that questions traditional providers; HolonIQ frames 2026 as "selective AI acceleration" rather than wholesale disruption; and GSV Ventures warns of a "hollowing out" of entry-level skill development as automation spreads.

The common thread: a shift from standardized, time-based, one-size classrooms toward personalized, competency-based, AI-augmented and lifelong learning, with teachers reframed as mentors/facilitators. These are forecasts and expert opinion, not established fact.

Partial 2026 forecasts · Open University, KnowledgeWorks, HolonIQ

Educating a child of Andrei's age (~6)

Warm-but-structured parenting, self-regulation and lots of conversation

The most consistent finding in parenting research: warm, responsive relationships paired with clear expectations and routines ("authoritative" style) predict better outcomes — warmth doesn't mean dropping structure. Reviews single out self-regulation and learning dispositions (persistence, curiosity) as the strongest predictors of school readiness.

Build language through rich back-and-forth talk — including about the past and future, not just the here-and-now — woven into daily routines, shared reading and storytelling. On screens, 2026 guidance favors managing (does it crowd out sleep, activity, homework, family time?) and high-quality co-engaged content over rigid hour caps. For developmental/behavioral concerns, structured evidence-based parenting programs are recommended.

Verified parenting-science consensus · Paediatrics & Child Health review, AAP HealthyChildren

6 Medicine

New discoveries & firsts (science-backed)

Landmark negative trial: pelacarsen cuts Lp(a) 78% but not cardiac events

Full results of the ~8,300-patient Phase 3 Lp(a)HORIZON trial were presented Nov 7 at the AHA Scientific Sessions and published simultaneously in the NEJM. Pelacarsen sharply lowered lipoprotein(a) but produced no reduction in major cardiovascular events after nearly five years, and caused more anaphylaxis. It is a major setback for the "lower Lp(a) → fewer events" hypothesis; Novartis is winding down the program.

Verified Nov 7, 2026 · Cleveland Clinic, TCTMD

First drug to hit Phase 3 endpoints in systemic Sjögren's disease

Amgen's dazodalibep (a CD40L-blocking fusion protein) met its Phase 3 primary endpoint in moderate-to-severe systemic Sjögren's disease, significantly reducing disease activity at week 48 versus placebo, with comparable safety. Sjögren's currently has no FDA-approved disease-targeting drug, making this a potential first; detailed data were presented at ACR Convergence 2026. Results are company-reported/conference-presented, not yet peer-reviewed.

Partial ACR Convergence, Nov 2026 · Amgen, Fierce Biotech

New mechanism: immune protein perforin kills tumor cells via iron-driven ferroptosis

A University of Michigan team reported in Cell that perforin — long thought to merely deliver cell-killing enzymes — can kill cancer cells on its own by disrupting iron handling, causing iron to accumulate in mitochondria and trigger ferroptosis. In mice, an iron-rich diet slowed tumor growth in a perforin-dependent way (researchers caution against generalizing to patient diets). Published ~Oct, just outside the strict window.

Verified ~Oct 2026 · Michigan Medicine, ecancer

President Nicușor Dan promulgated a law giving every pregnant woman the right to be accompanied by a person of her choosing throughout labour, birth and inter-institutional transfer, at public and private maternities alike, with refusal only on justified medical/epidemiological grounds.

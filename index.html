Dental medical discoveries

Machine-learning model flags high-caries-risk groups mainly from social factors

Researchers at NYU and the Federal University of Santa Maria (Brazil) modelled cavity risk across roughly 24,000 people and found that social determinants — income and household crowding — predicted decay better than clinical indicators. The approach is designed for population-level surveillance rather than chairside use, and largely describes existing disease rather than forecasting it. Published in Caries Research.

Verified·2026-11-09·Futurity · Oral Health Group · Dentistry.co.uk

Natural tooth protein studied as a route to regenerate dentine

Early-November coverage described research into a naturally occurring, highly acidic protein involved in dentine calcification, examined for its ability to help dentin-producing cells survive and mature. A related University of Illinois Chicago project received a ~US$1.2M grant to study pulp stem cells. The work is early-stage, with no peer-reviewed clinical result yet.

Enamel-mimicking gel reported to also rebuild exposed dentine

University of Nottingham researchers reported a material inspired by childhood enamel formation that also acts on exposed dentine, widely re-reported across November 2026. The underlying Nature Communications paper dates to November 2025, so this is recirculation rather than a new result, and the material remains unproven in the mouth with durability still to be established.

Adults — new finding (DSM-5 referenced)

Repeat theft in kleptomania linked to a heightened fear of financial loss

Kyoto University researchers report that people with kleptomania who repeatedly steal show exaggerated loss aversion and price sensitivity compared with repeat offenders without the diagnosis and with non-offender controls — pointing to a decision-making mechanism rather than a simple "urge to steal." Kleptomania sits in the DSM-5 under Disruptive, Impulse-Control and Conduct Disorders, so the work refines that category. The study did not measure brain activity directly and used a small clinical sample.

Verified·2026-11-24·Phys.org · EurekAlert · Frontiers in Psychology

Emotional abuse predicts later depressive symptoms via poorer family functioning

A longitudinal study of 1,887 Italians found that emotional abuse at baseline predicted worse family functioning and higher depressive symptoms six months later, with family dysfunction acting as a mediating pathway — mapping onto DSM-5 Major Depressive Disorder and its psychosocial risk factors. Note the sample is adolescents (14–17), not adults.

Partial·2026-11-27·PsyPost

Children — what develops in a ~6-year-old's brain (Andrei, b. 2020)

Concrete reasoning, early literacy and numeracy take off around age 6

Between 5 and 7, children move into Piaget's "concrete operational" thinking: they begin to grasp conservation (quantity stays the same when shape changes), tell time roughly, count and do simple addition, and start reading by sounding out words. Over the coming year, watch for a growing attention span and the ability to follow multi-step instructions. The CDC frames milestones as what ~75% of children do by a given age — checklists are screening aids, not diagnoses.

Verified·Evergreen (CDC/AAP/PBS)·CDC milestones · PBS Parents · Columbia Doctors

Friendships, fairness and self-regulation become central

Around 6, children increasingly want friends and to be liked, grasp rules and "what's fair," and begin managing big emotions with fewer meltdowns — though preschool-style fears (the dark, large animals, being alone) often persist. Over the next year, watch for stronger empathy, cooperative play and the capacity to lose a game without falling apart. Stable, nurturing relationships and play protect the developing executive-function system against stress.

Verified·Evergreen (AAP / HealthLinkBC)·HealthyChildren (AAP) · HealthLinkBC

Rapid vocabulary growth and conversational storytelling

By 6, most children speak in full, grammatically complex sentences, can tell a connected story, follow multi-step directions and understand thousands of words; vocabulary expands quickly through the school year. Flag to a pediatrician if speech is hard for strangers to understand or the child cannot follow two-step instructions.

Education

The next ~20 years vs. classical schooling

OECD: AI tutoring helps most when it questions, not when it answers

The OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 reports that AI tutors using Socratic, process-oriented questioning yield small-to-medium gains in subject knowledge and larger gains in critical thinking — but the gains vanish or reverse when the AI simply supplies answers. It also found that low-experience human tutors raised pass rates by about 9 percentage points when AI-supported, framing AI as reinforcing rather than diminishing the teacher's mentor role.

Verified·2026-01 (OECD outlook)·OECD (PDF) · Mexico Business

Personalization without challenge can backfire

The same OECD outlook warns that algorithmically "personalized" content without clear pedagogical intent can produce comfortable but unchallenging learning, and that over-reliance on these tools can reduce students' metacognitive engagement — their ability to monitor and direct their own learning. The 20-year implication is that design and teacher oversight, not the technology alone, determine whether personalization helps.

Partial·2026-01·OECD (PDF)

Educating a ~6-year-old (Andrei, b. 2020)

Protect and prioritize play

The AAP's clinical report on play urges parents to resist an over-shift toward pure "academic readiness," noting that playful, child-led learning builds executive function, problem-solving and social skills and buffers stress. For ages 4–6, pair structured activities (reading, early worksheets) with ample unstructured and physical play, and ask the school about recess and playful-learning approaches.

Verified·Evergreen (AAP clinical report)·HealthyChildren (AAP) · MDedge

Keep reading together, interactively

The AAP's 2024 literacy policy recommends shared reading from birth through at least kindergarten; at 6, let the child sound out words and take turns reading while you discuss the story. If using screens or audiobooks, the AAP advises pairing them with genuine parent-child conversation rather than passive consumption.

Verified·2024 (AAP literacy policy)·PubMed (AAP policy) · PBS Parents

Coach feelings, keep predictable routines

Because 6-year-olds are learning to manage frustration, friendships and fairness, name emotions, model calm problem-solving and keep consistent routines for sleep, meals and homework; expect lingering fears and occasional big reactions as normal. Stable, nurturing adult relationships are repeatedly identified as the single strongest protective factor for healthy brain and executive-function development.

Verified·Evergreen (AAP / HealthLinkBC)·HealthLinkBC · HealthyChildren (AAP)

Discoveries & firsts

FDA approves Ekitna (bezuclastinib) for advanced GIST — first new option in 20+ years

The FDA approved bezuclastinib (Ekitna, Cogent Biosciences) with sunitinib for adults with advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumours previously treated with imatinib, clearing it six days ahead of its 30 Nov target. The approval rests on the Phase 3 PEAK trial, which roughly doubled progression-free survival versus sunitinib alone — described as the first treatment approved in this setting in over two decades.

Verified·2026-11-24·Cogent Biosciences · AJMC · Pharmaceutical Executive

FDA approves Welgemda (ribitol) — first-ever treatment for limb-girdle muscular dystrophy

The FDA approved ribitol (Welgemda, ML Bio Solutions/BridgeBio) as the first approved therapy for any form of limb-girdle muscular dystrophy — specifically type 2I/R9 — as a twice-daily oral solution for patients weighing at least 30 kg. Approval was based on the Phase 3 FORTIFY trial (112 patients), with the FDA citing meaningful improvements in walking speed and lung function. Advocacy groups called it a decades-overdue milestone.

Verified·2026-11-23·Pharmacy Times · MDA · Neurology Advisor

First regional use of daraxonrasib, a first-in-class RAS inhibitor, for pancreatic cancer in the UAE

Burjeel Cancer Institute (Abu Dhabi) reported treating a 61-year-old patient with daraxonrasib (Rasonque), described as the drug's first clinical use in the region. Daraxonrasib is an FDA-approved, first-in-class oral pan-RAS inhibitor for metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma; its Phase 3 RASolute 302 data showed median overall survival of 13.2 vs 6.7 months versus chemotherapy. The "first regional use" claim rests on a single outlet, though the drug approval and trial data are independently verified.

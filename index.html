4 Psychology

Adults — a science-backed finding, mapped to DSM-5

Depression may be five distinct brain "profiles," not one condition

A University of Helsinki team used magnetoencephalography to map brain-network connectivity in 263 adults with major depression versus 75 controls, and found five reproducible connectivity patterns hidden under a single diagnosis, each with a different symptom load. DSM-5 link: it challenges the categorical construct of Major Depressive Disorder (296.2x/296.3x) and supports a dimensional/biotyping view; some clusters mapped onto PTSD and substance-use features. The authors call these candidate phenotypes needing replication — scans cannot yet guide treatment.

VerifiedOct 8, 2026 · Nature Mental Health
Sources: ScienceDaily · News-Medical · Neuroscience News · Psychology Today

Adult ADHD associated with higher later-life cognitive impairment

An observational study of ~187,000 adults aged 50+ in the NIH "All of Us" program linked an ADHD diagnosis to a markedly higher hazard of mild cognitive impairment/dementia, with higher ADHD polygenic risk tracking the same way. Importantly, the association weakened to non-significance when diagnoses were forced ~8–9 years apart (a reverse-causation concern), and the study does not establish causation. DSM-5 link: it connects ADHD (314.0x) with Major/Mild Neurocognitive Disorder — two chapters rarely bridged clinically.

VerifiedOct 7, 2026 · JAMA Psychiatry
Sources: JAMA Psychiatry · Medscape · News-Medical · Psychology Today

Children — what develops in a ~6-year-old's brain (Andrei, b. 2020)

Age six: executive function comes online; thinking turns concrete

Around age six the brain reaches roughly 90% of adult volume, and the prefrontal cortex is in active synaptic pruning and myelination — the period when working memory, impulse control and sustained attention begin to organize. Cognitively the child shifts from fantasy-based toward concrete logic: grasping cause-and-effect, time and quantity (counting to ~10, add vs. subtract), and becoming able to absorb formal instruction and read simple text. What to watch this coming year: emerging reading fluency and number sense; longer, more goal-directed attention (still only ~15 minutes); growing but still fragile emotional self-regulation; rising social comparison and sensitivity to fairness; and sleep and physical activity strongly shaping mood and attention. Milestones are ranges, not deadlines.

5 Education

The next ~20 years vs. the classic school model

Four scenarios for schooling — and a shift toward lifelong, personalized learning

The most credible forecast framework is the OECD's "Back to the Future of Education" (four 2040 scenarios): Schooling Extended (today's model intensifies with deeper personalization), Education Outsourced (learning moves to diverse providers and "learning markets"), Schools as Learning Hubs (schools persist but are re-purposed across physical and virtual spaces), and Learn-as-You-Go (school-based learning dissolves into ubiquitous, tech-driven lifelong learning). UNESCO's "Reimagining Our Futures Together" complements this by reframing education as a public common good built on rights, care and solidarity. Across forecasts, the common break from the age-graded, standardized, teacher-front model is personalized/AI-supported, modular and lifelong learning, with schools leaning toward social and competency hubs.

Verified (institutional forecasts)
Sources: OECD "Back to the Future of Education" · WEF / Schleicher (4 scenarios) · UNESCO "Reimagining Our Futures Together"

How to educate a child of Andrei's age (~6)

Play-based learning, short active tasks, warm consistent discipline

For this age the evidence favors play-based, hands-on and socially warm learning over heavy drilling: support emerging literacy and numeracy through daily reading, conversation and real-world counting; keep attention tasks short (~15 min) and active; and protect sleep, physical activity and unstructured play, which directly feed the attention and self-regulation systems maturing now. On screens, the AAP sets no fixed hour cap for ages 6+ but recommends consistent family limits, media-free times (meals, the hour before bed) and places (the bedroom). For behavior, the strongest evidence base is warm, consistent, positive discipline — the approach taught by parent programs such as Triple P, The Incredible Years and Parent-Child Interaction Therapy.

6 Medicine — discoveries & firsts

Childhood-to-early-adulthood antibiotics linked to higher IBS risk by 24

A study presented at UEG Week 2026 (Barcelona, Oct 17–20) found that antibiotic use from childhood into early adulthood is associated with higher odds of irritable bowel syndrome by age 24, with risk climbing as prescriptions accumulated (reaching ~1.7× for 6+ prescriptions in the Swedish BAMSE birth cohort of 2,853 participants). It is an observational association — no mechanism was shown, and the authors caution against avoiding necessary antibiotics.

VerifiedOct 18–19, 2026 · UEG Week

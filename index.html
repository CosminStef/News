Courses — market assessment (English-language online dental CE abroad)

How English-language online dental education is sold internationally

market overview · not time-sensitivePartial

Three sales models dominate: low-cost all-access subscriptions (e.g. Dentaltown's 500+ courses at ~$178/yr with certificates; ADA CE's 300+ courses), free sponsor-funded libraries used as brand lead-gen (Dentalcare.com/P&G, Colgate Oral Health Network, Viva Learning), and premium per-course or cohort "academy" brands from thought leaders (e.g. The Dawson Academy). In the US, compliance-driven topics sell best (opioid-prescriber rules, CDT coding certificates, sedation) because they are license necessities; growth topics include 3D-printing workflows, cybersecurity, GLP-1 oral-health implications and pediatric endodontics.

In Europe, clinical skill-building sells strongest — implantology, clear aligners/orthodontics, periodontics, occlusion, plus dental Botox/facial aesthetics. Note: ADA CERP accredits providers but does not endorse individual courses or instructors, which matters for credit when selling across borders.

src: ada.org · dentaltown.com · dentoflow.com

04 Psychology

Adults — recent scientifically grounded findings (DSM-5 linked)

Five distinct brain "profiles" in major depression

2026-09 / 2026-10Verified

A University of Helsinki team used magnetoencephalography to map functional connectivity in 263 people with major depressive disorder and 75 controls, identifying five biologically distinct profiles — some hyperconnected (one tied to severe substance use), others weakly connected (one with more prominent PTSD symptoms). The authors argue depression is not biologically uniform, which could explain conflicting past results; the work is preliminary and aimed at future biomarker-guided treatment. DSM-5: major depressive disorder, PTSD, substance-use disorders.

src: news-medical.net · neurosciencenews.com

Loneliness and isolation cost years of mental-disorder-free life

2026-09 · Nature CommunicationsVerified

A Tulane-led analysis of 277,000+ UK Biobank participants found that people who were both lonely and socially isolated lost substantial disease-free lifespan, mostly in mental health — men about 5.8 and women about 3.7 years free of disorders such as depression and anxiety. Healthy behaviours partly offset objective isolation but not subjective loneliness. The study is observational, so it shows association rather than proven cause.

src: news-medical.net · neurosciencenews.com

ADHD–mortality link in adults largely explained by anxiety and depression

2026-10-14 (preprint)Partial

A US National Health Interview Survey cohort (~52,000 adults) found adults with ADHD had higher all-cause mortality, but comorbid depression and anxiety accounted for roughly 58% of that excess risk — the ADHD–mortality link became non-significant after adjustment. The finding is a preprint, not yet peer-reviewed, and secondary reports cite inconsistent death counts.

src: adhdevidence.org · medrxiv.org

Children — the brain of a ~6-year-old (Andrei, b. 2020)

Brain near adult size; pruning, myelination, prefrontal maturation

developmental referencePartial

By about age 6–7 the brain has reached roughly 90–95% of its adult size. This stage is marked by synaptic pruning, increasing myelination, and continued maturation of the prefrontal cortex — the basis for better emotional regulation, longer attention and improved social interaction. Structural development still continues well into adolescence.

src: developingchild.harvard.edu · palomar.edu

Cognitive shift: emerging reading, cause-and-effect, fading "magical thinking"

developmental referenceVerified

Around age 6 children typically begin reading simple text, can count and start to write, grasp time and cause-and-effect, and sustain attention for roughly 15 minutes, while the preschool "magical thinking" fades. Over the coming year expect rapid vocabulary growth and a dawning grasp that words can carry more than one meaning (jokes, puns).

src: columbiadoctors.org · webmd.com

Social-emotional shift toward peers — and signs to watch

developmental referenceVerified

Friendships and group play grow in importance, cooperation and turn-taking increase alongside limit-testing and greater independence. In the coming year, consider a professional check-in for: loss of previously-held skills, persistent social withdrawal, trouble following simple instructions that disrupts school, or ongoing anxiety or aggression that interferes with daily life — single missed milestones are normal variation.

src: kaiserpermanente.org · cigna.com

05 Education

Education over the next ~20 years vs. classic schooling

WEF Future of Jobs: skills churn reshaping learning

2025-01Verified

The World Economic Forum projects that 39% of workers' core skills will change by 2030, with an estimated 170 million new jobs created and 92 million displaced (net +78 million). With ~85% of employers planning to upskill and ~50% to reskill staff, the trajectory points away from one-time classroom schooling toward continuous, AI-driven reskilling.

src: businesstoday.in · outlookbusiness.com

OECD "AI and the Future of Skills": what schooling must still build

project 2023–2025Verified

The OECD project assesses which human capabilities AI will struggle to reproduce, using an AI Exposure Measure that compares AI capability with occupational requirements across nine domains. Its implication for the next two decades: pivot education away from content AI can perform toward uniquely human capabilities — complex reasoning, collaboration and creativity.

src: oecd.org · oecd-ilibrary.org

Open University Future of Learning Report 2026

2026Partial

The report links education reform to AI and the low-carbon transition, projecting a shift from fixed classroom schooling toward personalized, lifelong learning. It cites estimates that AI could affect up to 40% of global employment and that around 39% of skill sets will change by 2030. Single institutional report; specific framing not independently corroborated.

src: iet.open.ac.uk

Educating a child of Andrei's age (~6) — evidence-based

CDC positive-parenting tips, middle childhood (6–8)

current (page updated 2024)Verified

CDC guidance for this age: show affection and recognize accomplishments; give age-appropriate responsibilities; help set achievable goals to build self-direction; talk about school, friends and the future; use consistent, clear discipline; encourage reading and active play; ensure 9–12 hours of sleep per 24 hours; and limit recreational screen time with a family media plan and no TV in the bedroom.

src: cdc (pdf) · chconline.org

UNICEF: learn by doing, encourage rather than punish

current guidanceVerified

UNICEF advises building skills and confidence by involving the child in real family tasks (setting the table, choosing clothes), relying on encouragement over punishment, and respecting the child's individual personality and pace rather than comparing them with others — supporting autonomy and intrinsic motivation at age 6.

src: unicef.org

AAP: parent as "media mentor," balanced screen use

referencePartial

The American Academy of Pediatrics frames parents as their child's "media mentor" and recommends a personalized Family Media Plan rather than a single fixed limit, cautioning that excessive device use can crowd out physical activity, hands-on exploration and face-to-face interaction — all central to a 6-year-old's learning.

06 Medicine

TLC590 long-acting ropivacaine hits primary endpoint in pivotal Phase 3

2026-10-19Verified

TLC BioSciences reported that its single-dose extended-release ropivacaine significantly cut movement-related pain over 72 hours after bunionectomy versus placebo and reduced rescue opioid use. It was also reported superior to the current long-acting standard, liposomal bupivacaine (EXPAREL), on both pain and opioid use, with analgesia lasting up to ~7 days. These are company topline results from a 304-patient trial; no peer-reviewed paper yet.

src: globenewswire.com · biggo.com

EyePoint's DURAVYU misses primary endpoint in second Phase 3 (LUCIA)

2026-10-19Verified

EyePoint announced that LUCIA, the second pivotal trial of its sustained-release TKI insert DURAVYU in wet age-related macular degeneration, failed to show non-inferiority to aflibercept on best-corrected visual acuity, though it hit secondary endpoints and cut treatment burden ~38%. Following the first trial (LUGANO) also missing its primary endpoint in August, this casts doubt on the wet-AMD filing.

src: globenewswire.com · biospace.com

Nature Medicine publishes Phase 1 of D2C7-ATC for recurrent glioma

2026-10-16Partial

A Duke-led single-arm Phase 1 trial delivered one infusion of the EGFR/EGFRvIII-targeting agent D2C7-ATC to 81 patients with recurrent malignant glioma. Median overall survival was 9.5 months, 11% of IDH wild-type glioblastoma patients lived beyond 20 months, three had durable partial responses, and toxicity was mostly grade 1–2 — encouraging signals in a disease with a poor prognosis. Exact publication date is ambiguous (Oct 16 vs 19).

src: nature.com/nm · scholars.duke.edu

Russia reports its first spinal cord transplant

~2026-10Unverified

Moscow's mayor announced that surgeons at the Sklifosovsky institute performed Russia's first spinal cord transplant — a 12-hour operation with a Chinese surgical team on a patient who had faced complete paralysis, reported to be recovering well. It follows a 1 September 2026 regulatory change permitting spinal cord, nerve and bone transplantation. Not peer-reviewed; no precise date or independent clinical outcome data.

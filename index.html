Adults — recent, DSM-related findings

Undiagnosed ADHD is common in women treated for depression and anxiety

VerifiedReported Oct 29, 2026

A clinical study found that nearly one in five women seeking treatment for anxiety or depression also met criteria for ADHD, and about half of those had never been diagnosed. ADHD was especially frequent alongside social anxiety and "double depression," pointing to systematic under-recognition of a DSM-5 condition masked by mood diagnoses.

PsyPost·ADDitude

Social-media use ranks among the top predictors of adult depression

VerifiedStudy 2026 · coverage Oct 29, 2026

An analysis of ~183,000 US adults across 11 annual surveys (2014–2024) found social-media use was the second-strongest predictor of depression after personal income, with its contribution turning positive around 150 minutes/day of use in every year studied. The data are cross-sectional and self-reported, so they show association, not causation.

PsyPost·HealthDay

Push to overhaul the DSM — editorial names five "blind spots"

VerifiedEditorial May 2026 · commentary Oct 2026

A Psychological Medicine editorial argues the APA's "Future DSM" roadmap risks adding complexity without fixing the core limits of psychiatric classification, identifying five structural blind spots and proposing a "dialogical" model in which diagnosis is a starting point rather than an endpoint. It runs alongside the APA's own announced strategy for a next DSM edition.

Children — Andrei (b. 2020, turning 6): the brain this year

The 6-year-old brain shifts from building to refining

VerifiedEstablished developmental science

Around age 6 the brain moves from forming connections to refining them: synaptic pruning removes unused links while myelination speeds signaling, making thought faster and more efficient. This frontal-lobe maturation underlies emerging executive functions — planning, attention control, self-regulation — and continues gradually through adolescence.

Child Development (Palomar)·Macmillan

What to watch over the next year (ages 6–7)

VerifiedCDC middle-childhood guidance

Expect progress toward reading simple text, basic logic and number sense, longer attention for tasks, and growing perspective-taking — understanding that others hold different views and feelings. Socially, look for deeper friendships, more independence and a strengthening sense of right and wrong, with emotional self-regulation still maturing. Persistent trouble learning to read or following multi-step instructions is worth raising with a clinician.

Education

The next ~20 years vs. the classical school

OECD "Back to the Future of Education" — four scenarios to 2040

VerifiedOECD, 2020 (horizon 2040)

The OECD sets out four alternative futures for schooling over roughly a 20-year horizon: schooling extended; education outsourced to learning markets; schools as learning hubs; and "learn-as-you-go," where school-based learning fades. They are deliberately fictional scenarios meant to stress-test today's standardized school model, not predictions.

OECD·WEF summary

OECD "Trends Shaping Education 2025" — AI literacy as the pressure point

VerifiedJanuary 2025

The OECD's triennial foresight report maps the economic, social, technological and demographic forces reshaping education, with a central question of how systems can deliver basic AI literacy to all learners. It frames classical, standardized schooling as under growing pressure to adapt.

OECD (full report)·OECD blog

Educating a child of Andrei's age (~6)

Favor "guided play" over purely free play for learning

Verified2024–2025 research

Evidence indicates guided play — an adult sets up a playful activity with a learning goal while the child leads within it — builds early reading and math skills more effectively than free play, even though many parents assume free play is best for learning. Practically: counting games, letter hunts and similar activities with a light aim, with Andrei driving.

The Star·Education Research Report

Interactive reading, consistent routines and protected sleep

VerifiedAAP / CDC / RCT evidence

Daily back-and-forth reading — asking questions and discussing the story — is a high-value practice at this age, and a randomized trial of coached read-and-play interaction linked it to fewer behavior problems. Pair it with consistent limits on screen media (so screens don't crowd out sleep, activity and reading) and 9–12 hours of sleep per 24h, which underpins learning and self-regulation.

Medicine

Daily peanuts improve brain insulin responsiveness in older adults (RCT)

VerifiedPublished Sept 30 · coverage Oct 30, 2026

A Maastricht University randomized crossover trial found that eating 60g of unsalted peanuts daily for 16 weeks improved insulin responsiveness in memory-related brain regions of healthy adults aged 60–75, with a companion study reporting small gains in cerebral blood flow and verbal memory. The sample was small (31 healthy participants), so it does not demonstrate dementia prevention. This is the freshest item squarely inside the window.

Euronews·ARY News

First positive Phase 3 for a personalized mRNA cancer vaccine (melanoma)

VerifiedESMO 2026, ~Oct 23–27

The Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial of an individualized mRNA neoantigen therapy (intismeran autogene) plus pembrolizumab in resected stage IIB–IV melanoma cut the risk of recurrence or death by about 42% versus pembrolizumab alone, with 24-month recurrence-free survival of ~82% vs 70%. Overall-survival data remain immature, but this is the first Phase 3 confirmation of the personalized mRNA cancer-vaccine approach.

MSK·Pharmaceutical Journal·Targeted Oncology

FDA approves talazoparib + enzalutamide for first-line HRR-mutated prostate cancer

Verified~Oct 26, 2026

The FDA approved the PARP-inhibitor combination talazoparib plus enzalutamide for adults with HRR gene-mutated metastatic prostate cancer in the first-line setting, based on the randomized TALAPRO-3 trial. This extends a combination previously cleared only in castration-resistant disease into an earlier line of treatment.

FDA·ASCO Post

Large cohort links regular tea drinking to lower vascular-disease risk

Verified~Oct 21, 2026

A prospective study of ~486,000 Chinese adults found regular tea drinkers had about an 11% lower risk of vascular disease, strongest for black and moderate-strength tea — but habitually drinking "burning hot" tea was tied to a 16% higher risk. The study is observational and cannot establish causation.

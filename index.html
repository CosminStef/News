Dental medical discoveries

Silver diamine fluoride halts cavities in baby teeth — no drilling

A University of Michigan-led Phase III randomized trial found that 38% silver diamine fluoride (SDF) painted on primary teeth every six months arrested decay in more than half of affected baby teeth versus placebo, with no safety concerns. The trial enrolled 830 children under six and was stopped early after meeting its primary endpoint, generating the clinical data needed for an FDA caries-drug application. The silver permanently darkens the treated area.

Verified JAMA Pediatrics 27 Jul 2026; re-covered 25 Oct 2026

A systematic review and meta-analysis in the World Journal of Meta-Analysis concluded that gut microbiota imbalance is associated with greater periodontal disease severity, based on literature through January 2026. 4 · Psychology

Adults — a science-backed finding (referenced to DSM-5)

Adult ADHD associated with higher later-life dementia risk

A University of Pittsburgh study in JAMA Psychiatry analyzed records and genetic data from more than 187,000 adults aged 50+ in the NIH All of Us program. Raw rates of dementia/cognitive impairment were 6.7% for those with ADHD versus 3.4% without; after adjustment the risk was reported as more than quadrupled, and people with the highest ADHD polygenic scores had ~35% greater risk even without a diagnosis. ADHD is a DSM-5 diagnosis; the study is observational (association, not causation) and does not establish whether treatment changes risk.

Verified Published 7 Oct 2026
UPMC Neuroscience News

"Adaptive aging" — reframing memory change in later life

A paper in Perspectives on Psychological Science argues later-life cognitive change is partly reorganization rather than pure decline: fluid abilities (fast processing of novel information) decline while crystallized abilities (accumulated knowledge, vocabulary, expertise) stay stable or grow. The authors explicitly separate healthy aging from disease processes such as Alzheimer's — relevant to DSM-5 Mild/Major Neurocognitive Disorder framing.

Partial Reported ~19–20 Oct 2026
Neuroscience News

Children — the ~6-year-old brain (Andrei, b. 2020)

Self-regulation is still a work in progress

By age six the brain is well past peak synapse formation, and pruning of the prefrontal cortex is underway but continues into adolescence. Executive-function and self-regulation skills are built through practice and supportive relationships, not yet fully formed — so difficulty managing frustration and disappointment at six is developmentally expected. Over the coming year, watch for growth in impulse control, waiting and turn-taking, and following multi-step instructions.

Verified Established developmental science
J Neurosci Columbia Doctors

Cognitive leap: memory, attention, early literacy and numeracy

Expect improving memory and attention span, the ability to follow multi-step instructions, and the emergence of basic reading, writing and simple math — the typical window where formal school learning begins to "click." Watch for persistent difficulty with basic reading, writing or math concepts, which is worth raising with a pediatrician or teacher.

How to educate a ~6-year-old (evidence-based)

Don't rush formal academics

A University of Cambridge evidence review on school starting age found children who began formal reading instruction at five did not read better long-term than those starting at seven, and by age eleven the early-start group showed less positive attitudes to reading and weaker comprehension. The practical takeaway is to favor rich oral language, being read to, and readiness over pushing early formal drill.

Verified Cambridge research review
Cambridge (PDF)

Play-based learning and emotion coaching

Peer-reviewed reviews argue social and dramatic play builds intellectual and emotional self-regulation and is linked to emerging literacy — a learning mechanism at this age, not a distraction. Because self-control is still developing at six, evidence-aligned practice is to name feelings, stay calm so the child can co-regulate, allow all emotions while setting kind limits on behavior, coach after big feelings pass, and keep routines and sleep steady. Harvard's parenting-for-flourishing work points to the combination of warmth, clear structure and autonomy support.

New discoveries & firsts (science-backed)

Personalized mRNA melanoma vaccine succeeds in Phase 3

A personalized mRNA cancer vaccine (intismeran autogene / mRNA-4157, from Moderna and Merck) combined with pembrolizumab cut the risk of melanoma recurrence or death by 42% versus pembrolizumab alone in the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial (1,137 patients with resected high-risk stage IIB–IV disease). About 82% of vaccinated patients were recurrence-free at two years versus 70% with immunotherapy alone — the first Phase 3 success for an individualized cancer vaccine.

01 Dental

Medical discoveries

Pure cranberry juice curbed plaque regrowth and acid output

A University of Illinois Chicago crossover trial (22 participants) compared 100% unsweetened cranberry juice, a 27% cranberry cocktail and water. The pure juice reduced plaque regrowth and significantly lowered plaque acid production. The 30-minute test window means it does not yet prove cavity prevention; the authors call for longer studies.

Verified Oct 21–29, 2026 (not strictly 24–48h) · News-Medical, Medical Dialogues (Scientific Reports)

Why implant infections resist antibiotics: titanium particles, not just bacteria

A Rutgers group (PNAS Nexus) argues peri-implantitis resists antibiotics because corroding titanium releases particles that impair immune macrophage clearance of bacteria — shifting the blame from bacterial resistance to the implant surface itself. It supports moving away from metal scalers for implant maintenance.

Verified Published Mar 2026, re-surfaced in Oct roundups (background) · Dentistry.co.uk, Medical Dialogues

Disrupting bacterial "quorum sensing" may rebalance plaque

Researchers reported that interfering with the chemical signals oral bacteria use to coordinate can shift dental plaque toward a healthier microbial balance — a possible non-antibiotic route to caries and periodontal prevention.

Partial Oct 6, 2026 · ScienceDaily (single roundup)

04 Psychology

Adults (DSM-5 related)

Drug ads can nudge AI models toward the advertised medication

Mount Sinai researchers gave 12 major AI models identical physician-written clinical cases, varying only whether a labeled drug ad preceded the case. An ad shifted treatment recommendations toward the advertised drug (roughly 35% → ~48% of responses). The authors stress it does not show the models recommended unsafe care, and they did not test effects on real prescriptions.

Verified Nov 11, 2026 · News-Medical, Medical Economics (Lancet Digital Health)

Common mental disorders are lasting longer than a decade ago

Using Dutch NEMESIS cohort data, a study in Psychological Medicine compared people surveyed in 2007 versus 2019 and reassessed three years later. Three-year persistence of any disorder rose from 42.7% to 57.9% (anxiety 24.4% → 40.1%; mood 33.5% → 48.8%), suggesting rising prevalence is driven by longer illness duration, not only new cases. It is single-country data measuring persistence over three years, not lifetime duration.

Verified Recent 2026 (not pinned to 24–48h) · PsyPost, ARY News

APA November 2026 journal highlights

Children — the ~6-year-old brain (for Andrei, b. 2020)

What is developing now

At around age 6 the prefrontal cortex is maturing rapidly, underpinning executive function — planning, reasoning, impulse control, prioritizing and decision-making. Synaptic pruning (removing unused connections while strengthening used ones) makes the brain more efficient and enables more complex skills, self-regulation and sustained attention.

Verified Evergreen developmental science · Palomar — Child Development

What to watch for in the coming year

Age-typical signs to expect: playing with other children and showing friend preferences; following rules in simple games; holding a pencil with fingers rather than a fist; dressing with little help; following multi-step instructions with improving memory and attention. Raise persistent difficulties with attention, impulse control, emotional regulation, speech or social reciprocity with a pediatrician — every child progresses at their own pace. Practical tools: the AAP developmental-milestone checklists and the CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early." materials.

05 Education

The next ~20 years vs. classic schooling

OECD Future of Education & Skills 2030 / Learning Compass

OECD's flagship framework argues future schooling must center transferable competencies — cognitive/meta-cognitive, social-emotional and practical skills — plus "student agency," rather than fixed content delivery. The Learning Compass is explicitly a framework, not a test or curriculum, and the OECD's "Trends Shaping Education" work pushes strategic foresight for systems.

Verified Official project (ongoing) · OECD

Open University — Future of Learning Report 2026

The report argues lifelong, flexible learning is becoming essential as AI reshapes work, citing forecasts (traceable to WEF Future of Jobs data) that AI could affect up to ~40% of global employment and that ~39% of existing skill sets will change by 2030. It points to AI-driven personalization and predictive analytics as the direction of travel versus uniform classroom instruction.

Partial 2026 · Open University (single institutional report)

EdTech Hub — 10 scenarios for education in 2035

An expert-interview-based foresight exercise identifying ~14 trends and 5 scenarios for how AI and education systems may co-evolve over about a decade. Its scenarios approach (plausible alternative futures) is a methodologically transparent counterweight to vendor hype — note the wide disagreement across sources on timelines and adoption percentages.

Partial Foresight report · EdTech Hub

How to educate a child of Andrei's age (~6)

Early play-and-read parenting support improves later academic skills

A randomized program (Smart Beginnings / Video Interaction Project) that coached parents in pretend play and reading aloud, delivered through pediatric primary care plus home visiting, followed children to age 6 and found increased parental cognitive support and stronger children's academic skills — with the largest gains for families facing poverty. Published in Pediatrics (AAP).

Verified Pediatrics / NYU Langone · AAP, NYU Langone

Guided play and shared book reading

"Guided play" — child-led activity that an adult gently structures around a learning goal — is associated with stronger learning than free play or direct instruction alone. Shared/dialogic reading (reading aloud while the child predicts and answers questions) and systematic phonics are core evidence-based literacy practices for this age.

Partial NAEYC guidance · NAEYC

06 Medicine

New discoveries & firsts

Controlled test: drug ads sway AI treatment recommendations

A Mount Sinai study (Lancet Digital Health) ran a controlled test of whether a labeled drug ad preceding an identical clinical case shifts AI treatment recommendations toward the advertised drug — it did, from roughly 35% to ~48% of responses. It is a first-of-kind controlled demonstration of advertising influence on medical AI (cross-listed with Psychology above).

Verified Nov 11, 2026 · News-Medical, Medical Economics

US pediatric cancer survival restated

Coverage around Nov 10 restated US childhood-cancer survival gains. The firm figures: 5-year survival ~87% for 2015–2021 (up from ~63% in the mid-1970s), per the AACR Pediatric Cancer Progress Report, with a 2026 study in Cancer reporting ~81.7% 10-year survival for 2001–2021 diagnoses and persistent disparities by race, income and geography. This appears to be a news restatement of existing data rather than a brand-new report.

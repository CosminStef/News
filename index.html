Medical discoveries & research

October JADA spotlights the oral virome and the "oral-gut-brain axis"

The October issue of the Journal of the American Dental Association leads with a UCLA review of how viruses across the oral-gut-brain axis may influence oral health. It connects to UCLA's $20.6M NIH-funded Human Virome Characterization Center, one of five national centres with partners including Baylor, Mayo Clinic and USC.

Verified Oct 5, 2026 (grant from Feb 2025)

Sources: ADA News · UCLA Health

Pure cranberry juice curbed plaque regrowth; the sweetened version did not

A University of Illinois Chicago team ran two small trials (8 adults, 14 children) and found unsweetened cranberry juice cut plaque bacterial regrowth and acid production by roughly 44% and 33% respectively, while sweetened cranberry cocktail showed no benefit. The authors stress the trials were small and cavity-prevention is still unproven.

Verified Study Jun 23, 2026; re-reported Oct 21, 2026

Sources: Hygienetown · Oral Health Group

Growing 2026 literature on GLP-1 drugs and periodontal health

Several 2026 review articles examine how GLP-1 receptor agonists (the Ozempic class) affect periodontal inflammation, dry mouth and alveolar bone. The emerging consensus is that they may reduce periodontal inflammation, but bone effects and clear clinical benefit remain unproven.

Psychology

Adults — a DSM-5-related finding

ADHD self-harm risk peaks before medication starts, not because of it

An international study reported via BMJ Mental Health found self-harm among people with ADHD climbed in roughly the 90 days before medication was initiated and then fell after treatment began, across five health systems. The pattern argues the medication is not the driver — emerging symptoms likely prompt both the clinical visit and the self-harm. ADHD is a DSM-5 diagnosis.

Verified Oct 22, 2026

Sources: MedicalXpress · ADHD Evidence (corroborating)

Several schizophrenia symptoms traced to one weak, slow brain signal

An imaging study (25 patients vs 25 controls, reported in Science Advances) found a single neural signal that was weaker and slower in people with schizophrenia, with larger abnormalities tracking impaired audio-visual timing, disorganised speech and overall severity. It suggests disparate symptoms may share one signalling deficit.

Unverified Oct 22, 2026

Sources: MedicalXpress

Children — a ~6-year-old's brain (Andrei, b. 2020)

What typically develops around age 6 — and what to watch for

Developing now: cause-and-effect reasoning and basic time concepts; the preschool "magical thinking" fades; formal reading and writing begin; attention span and working memory lengthen; a growing theory of mind (understanding others' feelings with guidance). Friendships become real but unstable, and the child is both more independent and, at times, less secure.

Watch over the coming year (raise early at wellness visits if a pattern persists): ongoing trouble with letter sounds/phonics or avoiding reading; persistent difficulty concentrating or settling into school routines; strong school aversion, extreme reactions or recurring stomach/headaches (possible stress signals); ongoing difficulty with dressing, eating or fine-motor tasks. Normal variation is wide — a repeated pattern matters more than a single instance.

Verified Established developmental science

Sources: GreatSchools · Cigna/Healthwise · Sesame Workshop

Education

Education over the next ~20 years vs classical schooling

OECD's four scenarios for schooling to 2040

The OECD's "Back to the Future of Education" sketches four futures: schooling extended (schools keep growing), education outsourced (learning shifts to market/community providers), schools as learning hubs (reinvented, blending physical and virtual), and learn-as-you-go (school-based learning ends, with AI embedding learning in daily life). The OECD frames these as thinking tools, not predictions.

Verified OECD reference report

Sources: OECD · NCEE

UNESCO: AI will reshape, not replace, the teacher's role

UNESCO's essay collection "AI and the Future of Education" frames the next era around ethics, equity and human-machine coexistence, warning of an "AI divide" as roughly one in three people still lack reliable connectivity. Paired with its AI Competency Framework for Teachers, the thesis is a shift to a teacher-AI-student dynamic in which the teacher becomes more of a guide.

Partial Sep 2025 report

Sources: UN DESA · SMART Technologies

Near-term forecasts: AI moves from pilots to routine classroom use

2026 education outlooks converge on AI shifting from experimentation to measured use with clearer guardrails — short targeted AI tutoring to support (not replace) teachers, districts standardising on one or two approved AI ecosystems, and AI literacy becoming an expected graduate skill. These are near-term predictions rather than a rigorous long-range model.

Partial Jan 2026

Sources: eSchool News · THE Journal

How to educate a ~6-year-old (evidence-based)

What the evidence supports for a child Andrei's age

Consensus guidance: keep unstructured play central (it supports imagination, decision-making and problem-solving); prioritise shared reading and rich back-and-forth conversation, where the interaction is the active ingredient for language and literacy; manage screens with daily limits and screen-free zones, noting research that parent-child co-viewing and discussion matter more than raw minutes; and keep consistent routines plus plenty of physical activity to help the child adapt to school's demands.

Verified Established science (through 2024-2026)

New discoveries & firsts

Dolutegravir (Tivicay PD) usable for HIV from birth

Trial data (IMPAACT 2023, reported in Lancet HIV) support an FDA decision allowing dolutegravir for infants from birth up to four weeks weighing at least 2 kg, extending its use into the newborn window. In a phase-1 trial of 48 HIV-exposed newborns, drug levels matched adult effective levels with a comparable safety profile.

Verified Lancet HIV Oct 22, 2026; FDA Aug 25, 2026

Sources: MedicalXpress · Healio

First-of-its-kind trial: ultra-low-dose radiation for Parkinson's inflammation

Mayo Clinic launched a trial testing ultra-low, anti-inflammatory doses of radiation therapy for early-stage Parkinson's, using imaging-guided conformal arc therapy over five daily treatments. The single-arm study of about 20 invited participants aims to slow motor-symptom progression at six months.

Verified Reported Oct 22, 2026 (trial start Jan 2026)

Sources: MedicalXpress · ClinicalTrials.gov

First human bladder transplant shows sustained function (context)

The world's first human bladder transplant (UCLA/USC, operated May 2025 on a 41-year-old) showed normal sensation, spontaneous voiding and full continence with no rejection, and the one-year follow-up reported the patient thriving. Published in The Lancet in June 2026, so this is established rather than a last-48h item.

Verified Lancet Jun 23, 2026

Children — a 6-year-old's developing brain (Andrei, b. 2020)

At 6→7: cause-and-effect, time, and the fading of "magical thinking"

Around age six the brain shifts toward concrete logical reasoning: children begin to grasp cause-and-effect and the concept of time, and preschool "magical thinking" fades. Typical markers include ~15 minutes of focused attention and following three-part instructions. Learning lands best through hands-on, concrete play rather than abstract instruction.

Verified Evergreen (clinical consensus) Columbia Doctors Kaiser Permanente

What to watch in the coming year

Emerging reading and writing of simple words/sentences; a widening social world (friends and admired adults like teachers begin to meet needs once met only by family); and lingering age-typical fears (monsters, large animals). These are ranges, not deadlines — persistent lags in attention, language or social reciprocity are worth a pediatric check.

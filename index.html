1 Dental

Medical / scientific discoveries

Routine dental care linked to lower liver-cancer risk in cirrhosis patients

A Virginia Commonwealth University / Richmond VA team reported that among roughly 47,800 veterans with early-stage cirrhosis, those who had at least one dental cleaning a year had about 27% lower odds of developing hepatocellular carcinoma over two years, alongside fewer hospitalizations. It is an observational association, not proof that cleanings prevent cancer, but it adds to evidence tying oral health to systemic outcomes.

Regenerative endodontics: minced-pulp grafts in mature teeth

A Journal of Endodontics study described placing a patient's own minced pulp tissue (from a third molar) into disinfected, blood-filled root canals of mature teeth; across six young adults, all treated teeth showed favorable radiographic outcomes at 19–42 months. It points toward regenerating living pulp in adult teeth rather than conventional root-canal filling, though the sample is tiny.

4 Psychology

Adults — a science-backed finding

A one-hour cognitive exam catches impairment the standard 10-minute test misses

A Boston University team, publishing in Neurology: Open Access, compared a one-hour Neurobehavioral Status Exam with the widely-used 10-minute MoCA in veterans. Among those who scored "normal" on the MoCA, the longer exam identified mild cognitive impairment in ~40% and dementia in nearly 9% — and it worked when delivered remotely. The takeaway: brief screens can give false reassurance, which matters more as early-stage Alzheimer's treatments arrive.

Children — what develops in a ~6-year-old's brain (Andrei, b. 2020)

Executive function and self-regulation are coming online — expect progress with wobbles

Around age six the prefrontal cortex is maturing rapidly, building working memory, impulse control, flexible attention and emotional regulation. Harvard's Center on the Developing Child stresses these are actively built skills (not yet mature, and still developing into early adulthood) — so uneven, inconsistent self-control this year is normal, not a red flag. Supporting the behavior that builds these skills matters more than expecting consistency.

ReferenceDevelopmental science
Harvard Center on the Developing Child

Milestones to expect this year — and what to flag

Synaptic pruning around this age makes thinking more efficient, supporting a longer attention span and better problem-solving. Expect a firmer grasp of cause-and-effect and the passage of time, fading "magical thinking," emerging reading and writing, a strong interest in rules and fairness, and friendships that matter more; lingering fears (the dark, big animals) are still normal. Watch for and raise with a pediatrician: persistent trouble with emotional regulation or attention, early reading struggles (and possible vision issues as formal reading begins), or real difficulty with peers and rules. The guiding principle from pediatric bodies is to act early on a concern rather than wait — note that the CDC's official milestone checklists stop at age 5, so age-6 guidance comes from pediatric groups and clinics.

5 Education

The next ~20 years vs. classical schooling

The credible forecast: AI personalizes learning — it does not abolish schools

The two most authoritative recent surveys converge. The OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 frames generative AI and intelligent tutoring as a way to scale personalized learning, while warning the same tools can undercut the effort behind deep learning (performance dropped once AI access was removed) — it favors intentional use, building independent-thinking skills first, and process-oriented assessment. The World Economic Forum's 2026 report similarly casts AI as delivering personalization at scale and makes AI literacy plus critical thinking core competencies, repositioning the teacher as facilitator. Neither foresees schools or teachers being replaced; claims that they will be are not supported by either body.

VerifiedOECD Jan 2026 · WEF 2026
OECD·WEF (PDF)
Educating a ~6-year-old (evidence-based)

Four things that actually move the needle at this age

Pediatric guidance is consistent: (1) keep learning play-based — real back-and-forth interaction and play beat digital media for learning; (2) read aloud together, ideally daily, with responsive conversation to expand language; (3) pair writing with reading — keep paper, pencils and markers around, since each skill reinforces the other; and (4) be skeptical of "educational" apps, which typically teach only basic skills, and model reading yourself so it becomes a habit he copies.

6 Medicine

New discoveries & firsts (scientifically backed)

Semaglutide data in children aged 6 to under 12 presented at ObesityWeek

Detailed results from Novo Nordisk's Phase 3 trial of once-weekly semaglutide (added to lifestyle changes) in 165 children aged 6 to under 12 were slated for ObesityWeek 2026 (14–17 November, Washington DC). In the topline data released in September, 40.4% of treated children fell below the obesity threshold at 68 weeks versus 0% on placebo, with no safety signals flagged for growth or puberty. Important caveat: semaglutide is not approved for obesity in under-12s, and these data do not by themselves create a new indication; a peer-reviewed publication could not be confirmed by the editor.

Some antidepressants beat fluoxetine on long-term outcomes, large analysis finds

A systematic review and network meta-analysis pooling about 115 trials and 30,000+ participants reported that escitalopram, duloxetine and agomelatine were more likely than fluoxetine to produce a sustained long-term response, with escitalopram and duloxetine also better tolerated; in maintenance, ~62% on continued treatment kept a sustained response versus ~45% on placebo. Commentators urged caution: the head-to-head rankings rest on a smaller subset of trials and shouldn't be read as a prescribing mandate.

PartialMid-Nov 2026 (secondary coverage)
Science Media Centre (ES)·Fox News Health

Roche's oral breast-cancer drug giredestrant: FDA decisions pending (not yet approved)

Clarifying a widely-circulated claim: giredestrant, Roche's next-generation oral SERD, is not yet FDA-approved. Two applications are under review — early-stage/adjuvant (decision expected by 30 November) and, with everolimus, ESR1-mutated advanced disease (decision expected ~18 December). The advanced-disease data (evERA) showed a 62% reduction in progression-or-death risk in ESR1-mutated patients. Flagged now because an approval could land within days.

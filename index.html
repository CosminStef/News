4. Psychology

Adults — a science-backed finding, mapped to DSM-5

Depression may hide five distinct brain profiles under one diagnosis

A University of Helsinki team used magnetoencephalography to map brain connectivity in 263 people diagnosed with major depressive disorder plus 75 controls, and found five distinct connectivity profiles rather than one signature — each mapping onto different symptom pictures. The implication for DSM-5 is direct: the single categorical "major depression" label may bundle together several biologically different conditions, which could explain why depression neuroscience has produced conflicting results. Children — what develops in a ~6-year-old (Andrei, b. 2020)

Age six: executive function, reading, and more complex emotions

Around six, rapid prefrontal-cortex maturation drives executive function — longer attention, following multi-step instructions, and early cause-and-effect ("concrete-operational") reasoning as magical thinking fades. Literacy moves from letter-sounds toward decoding words (the window where systematic phonics plus rich oral language matters most), while more complex self-conscious emotions (pride, guilt, embarrassment) and a stronger sense of fairness and peer friendship emerge. Self-regulation is still immature and needs adult support.

Verified (established science)
CDC milestonesSesame Workshop

What to watch for over the coming year

Worth raising with a pediatrician rather than waiting: persistent trouble with attention or following instructions beyond age expectation; marked difficulty learning letters/sounds or early reading; little interest in playing with other children or ongoing struggle to cooperate; loss of previously acquired skills (regression); or emotions, fears, or anxiety far beyond peers. Milestones describe what ~75% of children do by an age — children develop at their own pace, but any missed milestone or parental concern should prompt a conversation at a well-child visit.

Educating a ~6-year-old (evidence-based)

Playful, guided learning plus the "science of reading"

Children this age learn best through a mix of free play and gentle adult-guided "playful learning," with academic and social-emotional growth tightly linked. Pair systematic phonics and decoding with rich oral language, storytelling, and daily reading aloud (the combination, not phonics alone), build early number sense through play, and balance consistent routines with real choice to support developing self-regulation. Reinforce the same reading and play activities at home, protect unstructured peer play, ensure physical activity and sleep, and keep screen time limited and high-quality.

6. Medicine

New discoveries & firsts

Chemo-free regimen improves outcomes in Ph-positive leukaemia (NEJM)

A phase 3 trial of 236 adults with newly diagnosed Philadelphia-chromosome-positive acute lymphoblastic leukaemia found that ponatinib plus blinatumomab — with no chemotherapy — produced an estimated 24-month event-free survival of about 86%, versus about 67% for imatinib plus chemotherapy. The results support a chemotherapy-free frontline strategy for this leukaemia.

Verified14 Oct 2026

FDA approves first new-mechanism herpes antiviral in ~30 years

The FDA approved Hovilpri (pritelivir) tablets for acyclovir-resistant or refractory mucocutaneous herpes simplex lesions in immunocompromised adults. An oral helicase-primase inhibitor, it is the first HSV antiviral with a novel mechanism in roughly three decades; in its pivotal trial 63% of patients achieved complete lesion healing by day 28 versus 34% on investigator's choice.
